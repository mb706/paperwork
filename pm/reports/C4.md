## Summary

**The renderer** (`game/src/render/`, 10 §6) draws the view model with WebGL2 in Survey's look, every stage in its one
assigned pass (10 §6.2's steps, §9.8):
- step 1, **terrain**, one quad per visible chunk: Survey's material textures sampled in world space; soft edges where
  each pixel takes the strongest tile type of a 0.55-tile blend of the tile masks, looked up at a position warped by
  world-anchored value noise (0.32 tile on 1.6-tile cells, lattice values from §15.7's position hash); crisp types
  (concretes, town tiles, the map's edge) with an inked kerb; water-lining at A3's eight distances (one device pixel
  wide, fading with distance and below 0.5×) and an inked shoreline, both from a per-chunk shore texture whose signed
  distance also decides water against land, so lines and materials agree; the woodland tint under drawable trees;
- step 2, **ground layers**: resources, floor entities (belts, undergrounds), decoratives (culled below 0.5×), ground
  items, then **belt items expanded from the gap arrays** over the cached line geometry (straight runs and arcs);
- step 3, **shadows** MAX-blended into a half-resolution R8 target, composited as world-anchored hatching at −45° plus
  a 6 % darkening (a premultiplied "over", so it needs no read of the scene), falling on ground, belts and items only;
- step 4, **objects**, sorted by (bottom y, x, serial) with a counting sort by tile row: entities by the `entity` and
  `inserter` programs, trees and rocks, the character's clips;
- step 5, **higher objects**: inserter arms and hands, held items, smoke puffs (render-only, from DYN state and the
  tick);
- step 7, **lights** additive into a half-resolution RGBA16F target (RGBA8 without the extension), then **one
  full-screen grade**: the warm stain by day, the cyanotype LUT (32³, computed at start-up) with the lights' reveal at
  night, blended by the packet's daylight;
- step 8, **overlays** after the grade: ghosts (a blueprint-blue tint), the deconstruction mark, F4's debug overlays.

Step 6 (air objects: agents) and step 9 (the colour-blind LUT) have nothing to draw in M0.

The world renders into an RGBA8 scene target that the grade reads. The batcher (10 §6.3) writes expanded quads
(20-byte vertices, u16 uv precomputed per frame id) into growable streams, uploads each used range into one of two
alternating buffers and draws with one static Uint32 index buffer of 2¹⁶ quads. The sprite manifest v2 compiles into
typed tables at load; the six drawing programs of §15.7 (`programs.ts`) pass all 65 cases of
`test/fixtures/sprites/cases.json` against the Python twin. Camera: follows the player, zoom 0.3–3× in ×1.1 steps,
F9 / Shift+F9, snapped to whole device pixels; dpr ≤ 2 × the render scale. Context loss: waits for the restore,
re-creates everything and replays the pages from the worker's cache; without a restore in 5 s, one new canvas; if that
fails, an error with a reload button. GPU memory (10 §6.13): each mode's allocation is computed from the manifest and
the formats, the highest mode within its budget is allocated (stepping down on OUT_OF_MEMORY); the M0 atlas takes
42.0 MB at High in BC7, 51 MB with targets and buffers at 960×540.

**The asset loader** (`game/src/assets/`, 10 §9.2, §9.7): the asset worker fetches by priority (six in flight; class 1
is what the renderer drew as greyboxes; High level 0 takes at most two connections while Normal files are missing),
checks length and SHA-256, retries after 1, 2, 4, 8 s then every 30 s, transcodes with the vendored Basis Universal
2.50 transcoder to the format the main thread chose, and keeps 64 MB of compressed files to replay after a lost
context. The main-thread loader turns the manifest into requests, re-prioritises them from the renderer's misses, asks
for High level 0 where pages are drawn with k > 0.5 (and for every page in idle time at dpr 2), and hands levels to the
renderer, which uploads them within 2 ms per frame. Until a page is resident its sets draw their greyboxes; a page
without level 0 samples its Normal levels (a per-layer LOD clamp).

**The wiring** (`game/src/app/world-view.ts`, `fake-mode.ts`): `/?sim=fake` now draws the fake sim's world under the
placeholder page, sending the camera's visible tiles as view requests and applying each packet with the request it
answers. `window.__render` drives it (on-demand frames, camera, stats, debug flags, context loss).

**Run it:** `npm run assets` (once), `npm run dev`, then `/?sim=fake` or the gallery
`/src/dev/gallery.html?scene=golden|golden-real|transitions|survey|single|bench` (CLAUDE.md "Renderer").

## Decisions and deviations

1. **Tile ids with a 3-tile border (38²), not §6.5's one-tile border (34²).** The warp (0.32 tile) plus the 0.55-tile
   blend reads up to three tiles out; with one tile the soft edges seam at chunk borders. 1.4 kB per chunk.
2. **The shore texture's red channel is a signed distance** (128 + 32 d tiles, negative in water), computed from the
   same blended water mask the shader's material choice uses, with boundary points projected along the mask's gradient
   and carried by two raster passes (Euclidean, no scallops between texels). The shader lets its sign decide water
   against land, so the shoreline, the lining and the materials meet exactly. About 1–2 ms per chunk, within a 3 ms
   frame budget, visible chunks first, then the held margin, so chunks are usually ready before they scroll in.
3. **The lining fades between 14 and 18 device px per tile** instead of A3's emulation's hard cut at 16, so zooming
   never pops.
4. **Tile types without a material** (the fake's M1 tiles on M0 art) draw a near material tinted by A3's wash
   (`style.ts` `TILE_FALLBACK`), or the paper grain of the water material; with no materials at all (the tiny atlas)
   flat washes. Dev-only until the asset build makes M1's materials.
5. **Woodland only under trees the atlas can draw.** The fake places trees whose species has no M0 set; tinting under
   invisible trees showed as chunk-shaped green patches.
6. **The `idle` and `mine` clips run on the clock, not from `MOVERS.base_tick`.** §15.7 says they advance from the
   mover's base_tick, but golden rule 32 makes base_tick the packet's tick, so the clip would restart every packet.
   Positions still extrapolate from base_tick (at most two ticks ahead; none in on-demand frames).
7. **An inserter missing from HANDS rests open over its pickup:** θ = dir/16 + ½ (behind it, as the fake swings),
   r = its arm's length rounded to whole tiles. §15.4 sends HANDS only for inserters not at rest and says nothing of
   the rest pose.
8. **Floor entities** are the prototypes of type `belt` and `underground-belt`; everything else is in the object layer.
9. **Ghosts** are the entity's sprites tinted blueprint blue in the overlay pass (no hologram shader yet); a building
   marked for deconstruction gets a red cross (Factorio's icon is not in the atlas).
10. **Smoke** (§9.8): three puffs per chimney point of `set.smoke`, a 2 s life, drifting east and rising, fading;
    stateless (the serial and tick seed them), drawn in the high layer and graded.
11. **Fake mode joins by name** (C1c open issue 4) and logs at info level, not warn, because every e2e test fails on a
    console warning. It also **derives belt connection masks** from neighbouring belts, because the fake sends every
    mask as 0 (each belt would draw as a lone capped piece); `fake-belts.ts`, never used with the real sim.
12. **The Basis transcoder is vendored** (`src/assets/worker/basis/`, the stock 2.50 build from
    `~/.local/opt/basisu-2.50`, Apache-2.0, 1.1 MB, SHA-256s in `basis.ts`). Its glue is a classic Emscripten script,
    so the module worker inlines its text (`?raw`) and evaluates it once; the WebAssembly is a hashed asset (`?url`).
    Biome ignores the folder.
13. **This transcoder cannot make EAC R11 from ETC1S** (`isFormatSupported` is false; `transcodeImage` fails). So the
    worker loads the transcoder at start-up and reports what it can make, and the renderer picks the first format that
    passes both its GPU probe and the transcoder: shadows fall back BC4 → R8. §9.2's EAC R11 step needs a transcoder
    build with it (the trimmed build is already a follow-up).
14. **Mode budgets** (10 §6.13): High ≤ 256 MB (the slice's; the full game's is 768), Normal and Low ≤ 192 MB.
15. **The view model lives in `src/render/mirror.ts`**: the renderer owns it in M0. The UI's hover and build previews
    (§3.4) will need it; `ui/` cannot import `render/`, so it moves to `shared/` or is passed by `app/` then.
16. **The packet walker** in the mirror re-implements `decodePacket`'s checks without allocating; a test feeds both
    the same mutated packets and requires the same verdicts.
17. **`tsconfig.assets.json` lists `src/assets/worker/protocol.ts`** in `files`, so the loader and the worker share one
    message type (the worker's project excludes the main thread's files and vice versa).
18. **e2e changes outside my spec:** `e2e/console.ts` ignores the four "GPU stall due to ReadPixels" warnings that
    headless Chrome's compositor logs for any WebGL canvas here (a page that only clears a WebGL2 canvas gets them too);
    `e2e/fake.spec.ts` loads `?render=none`, since it tests the fake sim and the renderer needs `npm run assets`.
19. **Placeholder English** for the lost-context error and its Reload button: the text canon has no strings for them
    (nor for §9.7's "Some art could not be loaded; retrying", which the loader only logs). See open issue 1.
20. **F4 and F5 are stand-ins** in `world-view.ts` (F4 cycles overlay sets; F5 toggles a DOM HUD) until the UI
    package's input layer and its F4 window own them (I §4.21).
21. **The gallery and its scenes are dev pages** (`src/dev/gallery.html`, `scenes.ts`): the golden packets, C6's
    preview scene with a lit keypunch (the fake lights only furnaces and lamps, so its keypunch never stains), and
    §6.12's frame-cost scenes.
22. **Doubles travel in typed blocks on the frame path, and chunk ids are small integers.** Measured with V8's
    sampling heap profiler counting the objects the GC already collected, the frame path allocated 0.26 MB (1× mid
    base) to 1.4 MB (0.3× late base) of short-lived numbers per frame. A heap-growth check could not see it. V8 boxes
    a non-integer double passed to a call it does not inline, and a number outside ±2³⁰ that is returned, read from a
    double field, or used as a Map key. So:
    - the SpriteSink takes its placement in an argument block (`args`), not as arguments;
    - the programs read their anchor from `AT`, and the batcher reads quad corners from `QUAD`;
    - the mirror keys chunks by `chunkId(cx, cy)`, a small integer (`ChunkView.key` keeps the wire's chunk_key);
    - sort keys are integers in 1/256 tile, hashes are written into arrays (`posHashInto`), and alpha tints come from
      a 256-entry table;
    - constant uniforms are set once per program.

    The programs stay line for line comparable with `manifest.py`, and the 65 cases still pass.
23. **Proposed additions to `pm/DECISIONS-IMPL.md`** (the manager owns it):
    1. *Main-thread hot paths allocate nothing per frame, as measured by V8's sampling heap profiler with the
       collected objects included* (the renderer now; the UI's per-frame code, the audio's main-thread side and the
       mirror's packet path when they come). Decision 22 lists the V8 rules the checklist needs. A heap that does not
       grow after a GC proves nothing.
    2. *Shadows fall back BC4 → R8* on GPUs without BC4, until a transcoder build that makes EAC R11 from ETC1S
       ships (the stock 2.50 build cannot; decision 13).
    3. *e2e tests ignore Chrome's "GPU stall due to ReadPixels" performance messages*: headless Chrome's compositor
       reads back every WebGL canvas on this host. Every other console warning still fails a test.

- **Tests:** `test/render/review-r1.test.ts` holds 18 tests for the findings. The GL side runs the whole renderer on a

- **Shown to fail on the reviewed code:**

    `pick.ts` that throws stands in for the module it lacked.
  - The odd-height browser test fails with the old shader mapping (556 differing pixels by day, 100 at night).

### The majors
1. **Terrain after a world replacement.** The mirror has an epoch, bumped by every reset (a FULL packet, a new state
   generation). When it moves, the terrain pass drops every cached layer, and the scene's mover cache and the fake's
   belt masks start again. *Test:* another world with water where the grass was, at the same coordinates, uploads its
   tile ids again and builds its shores.
2. **Anchor culling.** `render/bounds.ts` compiles each set's conservative box:
   - it covers every frame of every part and pose (shadows, lights, clips), an inserter's arm and hand anywhere in
     their sweep, and the smoke's drift;
   - per prototype, it is the union with its greybox's box and its footprint;
   - the scene chooses chunks by the view widened by the farthest reach of any set, and every candidate by its box
     against the exact view. Quads are still culled one by one.

   *Test:* a chest seven tiles west of the view, whose shadow reaches 4.75 tiles into it, casts that shadow.
3. **Ground-layer order.** Decoratives, resources and floor entities are three passes over all candidate chunks, in
   §6.2's order, into the one colour stream. *Test:* a decorative, an ore tile and a belt across a chunk border draw in
   that order.
4. **Readiness.**
   - `ready()` is gameplay's: the core's Normal files delivered and resident.
   - `settled()` (tests, screenshots) waits for every requested file to arrive or fail once, and for no uploads or
     shores left.
   - The fourth failure of a core file rejects readiness and shows the error with a Retry button (placeholder
     English; the UI takes it over through `onCoreError`).
   - Any file failing four times shows §9.7's quiet notice.
   - Retry resets the failures and fetches at once (a new worker message, `retry`).

   *Tests:* in the unit tests, the core error and its Retry, and a failed optional file that leaves the loader
   settled. In the browser, every High level 0 answering 404 holds neither `ready` nor `settled`; a core file failing
   four times shows the error, `ready` rejects, and Retry recovers.
5. **Memory-pressure fallback.**
   - **Allocation is transactional.** Any GL error fails an attempt, not only OUT_OF_MEMORY, and the attempt deletes
     every texture it created. The GPU's limits (MAX_ARRAY_TEXTURE_LAYERS, MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE) are checked first.
   - **Modes are tried downward.** If Low fails, §6.13's safety net allocates the most layers that fit (a binary
     search).
   - **Pages share layers through the page → layer table**, least recently drawn first. An evicted page's sets draw
     their greyboxes, and the loader fetches it again when it is drawn.

   *Tests:*
   - a failed second array (OUT_OF_MEMORY) and an INVALID_VALUE both leave no texture behind and step the mode down;
   - a GPU that fits two colour layers shares them least recently used;
   - an evicted page is asked for again.
6. **Context recovery.** Every allocation of the arrays is an upload epoch:
   - after a restore (or a step-down), the loader takes the new formats and levels (`reconfigure`), asks for every
     Normal file again (the worker's cache serves them), and drops the replies of older epochs;
   - the worker drops older requests;
   - an upload in the wrong format is dropped, no longer thrown.

   *Test:* a reallocation from a mode that skipped the colour page's level 1 to High asks for that level, and a late
   reply of the old epoch is ignored.
7. **Terrain caches.** The tile-id and shore arrays grow by doubling, their layers copied on the GPU, up to
   MAX_ARRAY_TEXTURE_LAYERS. Beyond that, visible chunks evict chunks out of view, least recently seen first. A chunk
   without a shore layer stays pending and is tried again. *Test:* nine water chunks in view, with caches of 4
   tile-id and 2 shore layers, all get both, and nothing is left pending.
8. **GPU accounting.**
   - `memory()` sums what is live: the atlases, the terrain caches at their capacity, the targets at their current
     size, every vertex buffer as last specified (both alternating sets), the index buffer and the LUT.
   - The budget is checked before a resize allocates the targets, and after uploads. Stepping a mode down reallocates
     the atlases and renegotiates the loader.
   - The mode choice reserves 16 MB for vertex buffers.

   *Tests:* 4,000 quads add their two buffer sets; a larger window grows the targets; a 7,000² window steps High down
   and renegotiates.

### The minors
9. **The LOD clamp above layer 63.** The clamp is one R8UI texel per layer: a texture per array, read in the vertex
   shader on texture unit 4. So any layer count works. An atlas with more pages than the GPU has layers uses the
   safety net. *Tests:* level 0 of page 64 updates its texel; 12 pages on an 8-layer GPU share 8 layers.
10. **The late scene.**
    - **Belt items in one pass.** Belt items are expanded, culled and emitted together. A line outside the view is
      skipped whole. For a straight run, the in-view interval of distances is computed exactly, and only those items
      are placed. An item key's icon frame and layer are resolved once per frame, and quads are written in place.
    - **Inserters.** Arms and hands reuse cos and sin, and copy precomputed uv words.
    - **No clock.** The frame reads no clock unless `renderer.timing` is on (the F5 HUD, the bench).
    - **Packets.** The bench adds the cost of applying a steady tick's packet (the BELT_ITEMS snapshot) to each
      scene's main thread.
    - **Result.** The late base went from 17.2 to 13.1 ms median (the build from 14.8 to 10.9 ms), plus 0.4 ms for
      the packet.
    - **Not done here.** Static chunk buffers stay the M6 follow-up, and the UI's frame cost is C5's to measure.

    *Tests:* a 400-tile line with 1,600 items in a 20-tile view places fewer than 120 of them, exactly where
    `expandLine` puts them; a steady frame calls `performance.now()` zero times.
11. **Odd backing heights.** A half-resolution target holds window y ÷ 2 of the full target, anchored at the bottom.
    The sprite shader (`uFullH`) and the composites use this one transform. *Tests:*
    - the preview scene at 540 and at 541 px agrees in its top 540 rows, by day and at night;
    - pixel-exact placement holds at 401 × 301, dpr 1 and 2.
12. **The UI's boundary.** `src/shared/world-view.ts` holds types and `chunkId` only, so `ui/` may import it. It
    defines the contract:
    - **The API.** `WorldViewApi`:
      - the camera as drawn, in the canvas's CSS px (`toWorld`, `toScreen`, `visible`);
      - `pickAt`;
      - the read-only `model`;
      - `zoomBy`, `setZoom` and `follow`;
      - `setHovered`, which becomes the view request's hovered_serial;
      - `onFrame`, `ready`, `retryArt`, and `devInput.detach()`.
    - **Ownership.** `attachRenderer(app)` (`src/app/attach-renderer.ts`) implements the API over C3's SimHost.
      C3 owns the lifecycle: worlds, generations, and the numbering of view requests (a re-sent request is matched by
      its new number). C5 owns input; the stand-ins detach.
    - **The view model and picking.** The mirror implements the shared `ViewModel`. Picking (`render/pick.ts`: the
      selection box turned to the entity's direction, the selection priority, then resources, then tiles) reads
      prototypes.json only, so it works without WebGL.

    *Tests:* in the unit tests, priority, a turned box, a resource, a tile, and nothing outside the world. In the
    browser, `toScreen`/`toWorld` round-trip and `pickAt` finds the drawn chest, at dpr 1 and 2.

   size there, so it cannot be tested here, and it is left out.

5. **Proposed for `pm/DECISIONS-IMPL.md`:** the UI reads the world only through `src/shared/world-view.ts`. The
   renderer owns the camera, the view model and picking; the app owns lifecycle, the UI owns input.
