# C7: Audio v0 (the engine port, mixer, SFX, working sounds)
Status: done

## Summary

**What exists now** (`game/`):
- **`src/audio/`, the audio layer.** It imports only itself and `shared/` (check-arch clean).
  - `music/`: the conductor, and the **Era I and Era II rule sets** ported note for note from A4's prototype, Era II with the consonance fix.
    - The instruments: a multisampler with velocity crossfades, sine, drawbar organ and Risset bell.
    - The music engine and scheduler: a 100 ms tick and 0.8 s lookahead; late notes are dropped and counted.
    - The director: the program, two era slots with crossfades, postponement while a pack loads, next and previous. Also the FDN presets.
  - `worklet/`: **one AudioWorklet** (`bank.ts` around `bank-core.ts`). It holds:
    - 48 music and 24 SFX sampler voices on Int16 samples, and the synthesised voices;
    - the music channel strips: trim, EQ, tape, tremolo, tape echo, hiss, pan and sends;
    - each era slot's room: a dual-mono FDN reverb, its return, the air shelf and the era gain;
    - the SFX buses' faders.

    Notes go into a preallocated 2048-note queue. Nothing allocates in `process()`.
  - `mixer/`: the native rest of the graph (low cut, glue compressor, music gate and fader, world gate, master fader, limiter), and the eight volume categories of I §4.20 with mutes. The 07 §20 hooks are `setVolume`, `setMuted` and `setSettings`.
  - `sfx/`: the SFX system.
    - The sound table from `sounds.json`; variation (variants, pitch and gain jitter); rate limits; per-sound voice caps and priorities.
    - Factorio-like attenuation and pan from the view.
    - Working loops from `AUDIO_WORKING`, by proximity and count; footsteps.
    - The event-to-sound map, and silence for historical events and for events more than 30 ticks old.
  - `loading/`: the codec probe (Opus, else AAC); one decode path onto the canonical 32 kHz timeline (fingerprint-aligned, Int16 in slices of at most 1 ms); packs streamed by era, with residency.
  - `feed/`: the simulation's inputs, read through **C1b's generated decoders**.
    - `gen.ts` reads a `decodePacket()` result's EVENTS and AUDIO_WORKING sections through the generated offsets and accessors, with no allocation per record, and maps the `Hud` projection to the music feed.
    - `encode.ts` writes packets with the generated writer, for tests and the dev page.
  - `system.ts`: the façade the app uses; `index.ts` shows the wiring. It covers:
    - preload, unlock under a user gesture, resume on return, the hidden-page fade;
    - reduced audio when the worklet fails;
    - game start and end;
    - `onRenderPacket(packet)` and `onHud(hud)`;
    - the view, pause, map view, settings, music keys and status.
  - `offline.ts`: the same engine in an OfflineAudioContext (renders, recordings, the offline load), or live (the CPU benchmark).
- **`src/dev/audio.html`**, the dev page:
  - start, the program and its keys, the sliders, every sound, a factory's SFX load;
  - a *CPU* section that measures the offline load (10 §9.4's gate for the user's laptop);
  - the hooks the tools drive (`window.__audioDev`).
- **The asset build** (`scripts/assets/audio/`):
  - `build.py` fetches the pinned CC0 samples, builds A4's ship packs and the SFX, and encodes Opus and AAC with content hashes into `public/audio/`. It writes `music.json`, `sounds.json`, `inventory.json` (sizes and gates) and the licence record.
  - `render.ts`: offline renders through the real engine in headless Chrome.
  - `analyze.py`: loudness, spectra and stems against A4's.
  - `master.py` (recordings), `bench.ts` (CPU), `smoke.ts`.
- **Data:** `data/engine/sounds.yaml`: the sounds, the event map, `working_sounds`, and the music feed's era table with its bus gains.
- **Assets:**
  - `assets/audio/samples.lock.json`: 332 raw files with their SHA-256;
  - `assets/audio/licences.json`: every published file with its licence and its sources (repository, commit, SHA-256);
  - `assets/audio/LICENSES.md`.
- **Tests:** Vitest (`src/audio/**/*.test.ts`) and Playwright `e2e/audio.spec.ts` (@audio).
- **A listening page for the 32 kHz decision:** https://claude.ai/artifact/XDaRFjpsqnZEpFk7qveyeT. It is private: the user can open it, and must share it before anyone else can.

**The branch:** it carries `dev` merged twice: C1b's generated decoders, then C6 and C1c. Against `dev` it adds only audio files and these shared ones: `CLAUDE.md` (an Audio section), `game/biome.json` (one ignore line), `game/data/engine/sounds.yaml`, `game/e2e/audio.spec.ts`, `game/public/audio/.gitignore` and `game/src/dev/audio.*`.

## Decisions and deviations

1. **The rooms and the SFX bus faders run in the voice bank, not as native nodes.** 10 §9.4 has an FDN worklet per era slot and native buses.
   - Measured in Chrome 156 here: beside an AudioWorklet, every native node renders on the worklet's thread, at several times its usual cost.
     - A GainNode costs about 0.1 % of a host core there.
     - Each further worklet node costs about 0.9 %.
     - Two compressors cost 1.8 %.
   - The graph as first built had the bank, two FDN worklets, 24 GainNodes, 3 biquads and 2 compressors. Silent, it cost 9.5 points of a core more than a bare context.
   - Now the bank runs, for each era slot, the FDN, the return, the air shelf (BiquadFilterNode's high-shelf formula) and the era gain (setTargetAtTime semantics, with `cancelScheduledValues`). It also runs the six SFX buses' faders.
   - The bank has three outputs: music, world SFX, and GUI and alert SFX. The native graph keeps the low cut, the glue compressor, the music gate and fader, the world gate, the master fader and the limiter.
   - The silent graph now costs 4.3–5.9 points above a bare context, against 9.5 before.
   - A render through the new graph nulls against the old one at −88 dB (Era I and Era II).
   - Reduced audio makes its per-bus GainNodes on first use.
2. **Recorded music joins after the low cut and the glue compressor.** The recordings were mastered through both already. `master.py` said so, but the graph routed them through the glue a second time.
3. **The FDN is two mono networks per room,** left and right with different line lengths, as A4's two-channel ConvolverNode had independent IRs.
   - A shared stereo network changed the reverb's level and colour with the input's correlation: +2–4 dB at 125 Hz–1 kHz, and +1.8 LU on Era II's plate.
   - Its presets are the decays A4's generated IRs actually have: about the mid T60 from 250 Hz to 8 kHz, because A4's band split never applied its nominal high-band T60. `fdn-core.test.ts` measures them per octave.
   - The network runs a block at a time, since every line is longer than a render quantum. The output is bit-identical to the per-sample loop, and 1.9× faster.
   - Sample voices that hold or release take a fast path, also bit-identical.
4. **Risset's bell is an additive voice in the bank,** not a pre-rendered sample (10 §9.4 pre-renders voices with many partials).
   - Era II plays two bells a piece, at pitches and lengths the rule set chooses, so a sample set would be large for little use.
   - In the bank, a bell is 11 table oscillators for a few seconds.
5. **The era bus gains are recalibrated** to 13.6 dB (Era I) and 10.7 dB (Era II), from A4's 13.9 and 9.4.
   - At A4's values, six game-length renders at 32 kHz, through the glue compressor, averaged −17.7 LUFS (Era I) and −19.3 LUFS (Era II).
   - The new values centre a piece on −18 LUFS at 100 % music (A4 §8). They live in `sounds.yaml`.
6. **Two small rule-set changes,** each commented in the code. Both stop a change of activity from replaying missed notes in a burst.
   - Era I: when activity rises after section A2 has begun, the harp arpeggio joins at the present, in phase.
   - Era II: while activity is below 0.3, the silent second marimba tape keeps its place, so it rejoins in phase.
   - Also, the hiss's fade-out time is clamped to the piece's start for very short pieces.
7. **Velocity layers:** a sampler note crossfades its two layers unless it is shorter than 0.5 s or the part is fast and percussive. This follows A4 §6.5 #2 and 10 §9.4's "one velocity layer for short and percussive notes". String notes longer than a sample are re-bowed with 1.6 s crossfades that skip the attack.
8. **All eight sound categories of I §4.20,** each with a mute: master, music, effects, GUI, alerts, environment, walking and ambient. The brief named master, music, effects and UI; 07 §20 adds none. Music defaults to 70 %.
9. **C1b's decoders are used, so the seam is closed.**
   - `dev` had them before I finished, so I merged `dev` into this branch, wired them in, and deleted the stand-in decoder.
   - A second merge brought C6 and C1c. Conflicts in `CLAUDE.md` and `biome.json` were resolved by keeping both sides.
10. **Files outside the listed scope:**
    - `data/engine/sounds.yaml`: 10 §15.6 names this file, and the schema reads its sound names and its `working_sounds`. That key was `working`; I renamed it to match `schema/protos.yaml`.
    - `e2e/audio.spec.ts`; `biome.json` (it ignores the generated `public/audio/`, as it does `public/data/`); `public/audio/.gitignore`.
    - Node tools beside the Python build in `scripts/assets/audio/`: `render.ts`, `bench.ts`, `smoke.ts` and `proto-fixtures.mjs`.
11. **The build names files by a hash of the master's samples,** not of its bytes. libsndfile stamps a float WAV's PEAK chunk with the time of writing, so every rebuild had renamed every SFX file. Names are now stable across rebuilds.
12. **The error sound has one variant.** A4 made two, but its generator ignores the variant, so they were identical files.
13. **Firefox:**
    - OfflineAudioContext has no `suspend()` there, so an offline render schedules all its ticks up front against a simulated clock; the bank's queue holds the notes.
    - Headless Firefox here has no audio device, so a realtime context never leaves `suspended`. Its three realtime e2e tests skip, with that reason.
    - Playwright's Firefox build has no AAC decoder, so its decode test covers Opus only.
14. **The autoplay-refusal test simulates the policy.** Chrome for Testing runs Web Audio without a user activation here, headless and headed under Xvfb (measured). So the test holds back `AudioContext.resume()` and `HTMLMediaElement.play()` until a trusted gesture, as browsers document the policy.

## Verification

**CPU** (10 §9.4, §11). Method: `bench.ts`.
- Chrome 156 was pinned to socket 0. Each configuration played live for 140 s from the piece's start, in two rounds run in opposite orders.
- The audio threads' CPU was read from `/proc` every second.
- The SFX load: 12 working loops for 100 machines in view, 6 one-shots a second, and a burst of 20 builds every 5 s. It saturates the 24 SFX voices, stealing about 95 a run.
- No note was dropped. The host's load average was about 13 during the first round (other agents) and about 1 at the end.

| Configuration | Host: mean | Host: worst second | Reference (÷ 2.4): mean / worst | Budget (reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Era I + SFX load** | **16.1 %** | 20.4 % | **6.7 % / 8.5 %** | ≤ 8 % / ≤ 15 % |
| **Era II + SFX load** | **17.7 %** | 23.9 % | **7.4 % / 10.0 %** | ≤ 8 % / ≤ 15 % |
| Era I music only | 12.4 % | 22.6 % | 5.2 % / 9.4 % | |
| Era II music only | 15.3 % | 20.8 % | 6.4 % / 8.7 % | |
| SFX load only | 10.6 % | 14.5 % | 4.4 % / 6.0 % | |
| The graph, silent | 6.1 % | | | |

- **Both eras meet the budget** (host ≤ 19 % on average, ≤ 36 % in any second), which covers M0's exit (§12). Era II's average has 1.3 points of margin on the host.
- B2's shipping graph measured Era II with SFX at 22.4 %, under a lighter SFX load (8 loops). The synthesised notes, echo and hiss now run in the bank, as do the rooms and bus faders.
- **Main thread:** at most 0.2 % of a core on average. The longest tick was 4.2 ms once (Era I), otherwise at most 3.1 ms; that is ≤ 1.8 ms on the reference, inside 2 ms.
- **Offline load**, the method of M1's gate on the user's laptop (the dev page's *CPU* section):

  | Configuration | Mean | Worst second | Reference (÷ 2.4) | Gate (reference) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Era I + SFX | 5.4 % | 7.6 % | 2.3 % / 3.2 % | ≤ 3.5 % / ≤ 6.5 % |
  | Era II + SFX | 6.5 % | 9.6 % | 2.7 % / 4.0 % | ≤ 3.5 % / ≤ 6.5 % |

  Both are inside the gate.
- **Caveat: the clock.** The intel_pstate governor ran the audio threads at 1.3–1.5 GHz, measured from the clock of the core each thread ran on, weighted by its CPU time. §11's ÷ 2.4 assumes the 2.6 GHz base clock. Scaled to 2.6 GHz, the realtime averages would be 8.3 % (Era I + SFX) and 9.6 % (Era II + SFX) of a host core.
  - Offline renders run flat out at turbo clocks. That is why realtime is 2.7–3.0 times the offline load here, against B2's 1.9–2.1.
  - While the output is silent, Chrome suspends it (its output threads stop). The silent-graph row therefore lacks about 3 points that a playing graph pays.
- **What to cut if a machine misses the budget,** in order of what the ear loses:
  1. `latencyHint: 'playback'`: −3.5 points measured on the silent graph, with no change to the sound, but longer output latency for the SFX. This needs a decision.
  2. 16 SFX voices instead of 24 under load.
  3. One tape insert per Era II bus instead of per channel (§9.4 step 2).
  4. A 32-voice music cap (§9.4 step 3).

  Machines that still fail get reduced audio (§9.4).
- Profile of the bank's own work in Node: the FDN was a third of it before the block rewrite; voices about a quarter, the strips an eighth.

**The 32 kHz engine against 48 kHz and A4's demos.** These are the same notes, engine renders against A4's renders. Each cell is the share of the whole piece's power in that band, in dB:

| | 4–8 kHz | 8–12 kHz | 12–16 kHz | 16–24 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Era I, A4's demo | −22.8 | −42.0 | −51.7 | −59.1 |
| Era I, engine 48 kHz | −23.6 | −43.3 | −51.0 | −44.3 |
| Era I, engine 32 kHz | −24.1 | −43.3 | −55.2 | none |
| Era II, A4's demo | −34.0 | −44.1 | −53.4 | −64.3 |
| Era II, engine 48 kHz | −35.4 | −44.9 | −54.2 | −62.9 |
| Era II, engine 32 kHz | −35.6 | −45.4 | −61.8 | none |

- Up to 12 kHz the three agree within 1.5 dB. Before matching, the excerpts' loudness differed by at most 0.5 LU.
- The game decodes samples at 32 kHz at either engine rate. Era I's extra power above 16 kHz at 48 kHz is the interpolation's images of those samples, not music.
- Era II's hiss is generated, so at 32 kHz it falls away above about 10 kHz.
- The listening page (link above) has loudness-matched excerpts and a blind test (10 rounds; 9 or more right means the user hears a difference).

**Fidelity to A4's approved demos**
- **Note for note:** `pieces.test.ts` runs seven configurations through the TypeScript rule sets: both eras, several seeds, transpositions and activity curves. Every note matches golden digests made by A4's JavaScript prototype (`scripts/assets/audio/proto-fixtures.mjs`). The conductor reproduces the prototype's programs for two seeds and for a 12.6 h game (`conductor.test.ts`).
- **Harmony:**
  - Era I has no sustained minor second or ninth below C5 (A4's criteria).
  - Era II's organ and its echo stay consonant: dissonant under 5 % of the time (the round-1 fix).
  - Era II's echo is erased at every drone change.
- **Renders against A4's:** the engine at 48 kHz against A4's ship-pack render of Era I (seed 1945) and A4's final render of Era II (seed 1962).
  - The spectrograms show the same piece, event for event.
  - The long-term 1/3-octave spectra agree within about 2 dB from 63 Hz to 8 kHz.
  - Short-term loudness tracks the reference throughout. I inspected the pictures: `.cache/assets/audio/renders/cmp-era*-48.png`.
  - Stems: Era I's channels are within ±0.2 LU of A4's, and its reverb within +0.4 LU. Era II's are within ±0.2 LU, except the vibraphone (−0.7) and the plate (−1.2), where A4's demo used the full pack.
- **Loudness:**
  - At the calibrated bus gains, a game-length piece measures −18 LUFS at 100 % music.
  - The charter recording is mastered to −18.0 LUFS and −1.92 dBTP (153.4 s, 2.0 MB Opus).
  - The e2e test renders the charter piece and requires −18 ± 1.5 LUFS, no clipping and no denormals. It passes in Chrome and Firefox.
- **Offline renders** report a peak of 0.66–0.75 at 32 kHz, 0 denormals and 0 non-finite samples.

**No clicks**
- The bank's tests:
  - A hard stop is a 3 ms fade whose steps stay below the signal's own.
  - A steal at the 48-voice cap fades the quietest voice, with steps 1/96 of a cut.
  - A loop wraps at its manifest points without a seam.
  - `clear()` fades a slot within its time.
  - The rooms' era gain follows setTargetAtTime and `cancelScheduledValues`, and a bus fader that moves while the bus is silent arrives at its value.
- The e2e test decodes every file of both packs and the SFX onto the canonical timeline (length and fingerprint): Opus, and AAC in Chrome. It plays every loop three times through the bank. No step at a join exceeds 1.5 times the signal's 99th-percentile step; spectral flux at the joins is logged beside four reference points.

**Tests and checks**
- Vitest, with deterministic seeds:
  - `src/audio`: 9 files, 68 tests (rule sets, conductor, engine, scheduler, director, bank, FDN, SFX, feed, decoder).
  - The FDN tests compare each preset with A4's generated IRs: the level per Hz in the music band (within 1.5 dB) and the decay per octave (within ±12 %).
  - The feed tests check that what audio reads equals what the generated writer wrote, and that the generated strict reader accepts the test packets.
  - The whole repository: 32 files, 382 tests, all green.
- Playwright `e2e/audio.spec.ts` (@audio):
  - Chrome, 5 of 5:
    - start under a gesture with a clean console, playing the program, every sound and the load test (through `onRenderPacket`);
    - a blocked AudioWorklet falls back to reduced audio (native SFX, recorded music);
    - an autoplay refusal stays blocked until the next gesture;

  - Firefox: the render and decode tests pass; the three realtime tests skip.

## Open issues and follow-ups

1. **The 32 kHz decision needs the user's ears** (10 §9.4). Up to 12 kHz the renders agree; 32 kHz removes the band above 16 kHz, which in the demos is 59 dB (Era I) and 64 dB (Era II) below the whole piece's power. If the user hears a difference, set `ENGINE_RATE` to 48 kHz and lower the voice caps; the decode rate follows.
2. **M1's CPU gate runs on the user's laptop.**
   - The gate is the offline load of Eras I and II with the SFX load: at most 3.5 % on average and 6.5 % in the worst second (10 §9.4).
   - The dev page's *CPU* section measures it now. `bench.html` (Integration and QA) should call `renderOffline` the same way.
   - The clock caveat above applies to every ÷ 2.4 conversion from this host.
3. **Prototype facts for the event map** come from `prototypes.json`: an entity's footprint size, and whether `entity_mined` names a resource. The app passes them in as `proto`. Until it does, every built entity plays the small build sound. Developer fail reasons already come from the schema.
4. **The data stage should read `sounds.yaml`:** its `sounds` table and the `working_sounds` column are placeholders on `dev`. Ids are the sounds sorted by name, as `SoundTable` computes them.
5. **`npm run assets` (C6) should run `gamedev-python -I scripts/assets/audio/build.py` as its audio step.** `public/audio/` must exist before the dev page or the game can play.
6. **Biome fails on `dev`'s own `test/golden/golden.test.ts`** (C1c, `useIterableCallbackReturn`).
7. **M1c's additions:**
   - the slice's remaining ~15 SFX (A4 §7.4), in `sounds.yaml` and `sfx.py`;
   - the recorded pieces for reduced audio, made with `master.py`. Only the charter piece is recorded now.
