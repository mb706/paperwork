# A2: Multiplayer architecture
Status: done (revised after codex review r1; see *Review response*)

## Summary

`design/multiplayer/DESIGN.md` is a complete, implementable multiplayer design. It covers the brief's ten points, each with its own section, and every number is either measured in a proof of concept on this host or cited (44 sources in Appendix A).

**The architecture in brief.**
- **Model.** Deterministic lockstep, as Factorio does it: every client runs the whole simulation and only input actions travel.
- **Sequencing.** A small relay owns the tick clock and never simulates. Clients stamp each action with the tick they expect it to run in (estimated relay tick plus an adaptive margin). The relay accepts on-time actions, moves late ones to the open tick, keeps each player's actions in order, and broadcasts each closed tick's action list. This is Factorio's scheme from FFF-302.
- **No host.** Without a host there is nothing to migrate, and rooms (checkpoint plus tick log on the relay) outlive their players.
- **Durability.** The relay writes every closed tick to the room's log before broadcasting it, and opens a new room generation at every start, so a crash cannot fork the history.
- **Transport.** WebSocket over TLS on 443, same origin as the site: one control socket and one bulk socket.
- **No NAT hole punching.** From a browser it exists only as WebRTC/UDP, which the user's network (LRZ's Secomat: documented automatic host blocking on scan-like flow patterns) penalises. Even native hole punching fails for about 30% of attempts, and with a relay sequencer it would not shorten the critical path.
- **Joining.** A donor client writes a complete checkpoint after a sequenced marker tick and sends it as a verified transaction (immutable manifest, unique chunks, SHA-256), paced so its own game does not lag. The relay caches it, and the joiner fast-forwards the buffered ticks.
- **Desyncs.** A per-tick digest plus a key-chosen slice hash, with its lineage saved in checkpoints, reported every 30 ticks with an epoch. Verdicts are kept for the 60-s maximum lag, decided by majority vote. Repair is a background re-sync in a second worker, with a convergence check and a fallback to foreground repair.
- **Determinism and engine seams.** D1–D23 is a checklist for M0, each rule with its enforcement; D19 is A1's Rust/WASM rule set. S1–S15 are the engine seams, final for Phase B, including the new world-replacement protocol (S15). Single player is multiplayer with a `LocalSequencer`.

**Proof of concept.** `design/multiplayer/poc/` holds about 2,550 lines of JavaScript, with `ws` 8.22.0 as the only dependency. It lints clean under Biome 2.5.15.
- A relay.
- A lockstep client that runs in Node and in a browser worker, with a shadow re-sync thread.
- A toy deterministic simulation.
- A userspace impairment proxy.
- A scenario runner with 12 scenarios; it requires full canonical-state agreement and reports whether every player got into the game.
- Wire-level probes, a relay load generator, and a raw-CDP hidden-tab experiment in Chrome.

How to run it is in DESIGN §12.1.

## Decisions and deviations

1. **The relay sequences, not a hosting client** (brief point 1 left both open). The constraint allows sequencing, the relay never sleeps, and host migration disappears as a problem.
2. **Clients stamp target ticks; the relay arbitrates and enforces per-player order** (DESIGN §3.4, §12.2). Client stamping mispredicted 0–5% of walk inputs on LAN, home and far links; relay stamping 2–35% on LAN and home links and 32–54% on a far link, across four runs.
3. **No NAT hole punching, no WebRTC, no WebTransport in version 1** (brief point 2): §2.1–§2.3. WebTransport stays a possible opt-in later, never probed automatically, because its first attempt is QUIC over UDP. Safari 26.4 has an HTTP/2 fallback; for Chrome and Firefox I could verify only HTTP/3.
4. **Spec 10 §4.4's single `World.player` becomes `players[]`** (S1), with input generations and creation rings, and the save's `PLAYER` section becomes `PLAYERS`. A proposed change to 10, for Phase B.
5. **Stricter than 10 §4.1 if the simulation stays TypeScript: ban `**` and `Math.pow` entirely** (D5). ECMA-262 makes `Number::exponentiate` implementation-approximated for every exponent, and V8 constant-folds `**` differently. With Rust/WASM (A1), D19 replaces the TypeScript bans.
6. **10 §8.5's input log becomes the closure log, with its blobs** (S8), so replays, bug bundles and multiplayer share one format.
7. **10 §3.3 and 07 §19.3:** in multiplayer the game never pauses on a hidden tab. The simulation worker is driven by arriving ticks rather than timers (measured, §12.5), and background execution is treated as best effort.
8. **Chat is out of band** in multiplayer, unlike Factorio's `write_to_console` input action (I §8.2). Console *commands* that change the world remain actions.
9. **Selection is presence, not simulation state.** The UI result is Factorio's: other players' coloured selection rectangles, as in 2.0.33.
10. **Client autosaves on by default** (Factorio's `autosave_only_on_server` is true), because our relay cannot save without a donor and any player must be able to re-host.
11. **Remote cursors proposed as a BEND, off by default**, and listed as a question for the user.
12. **A1's action format adopted** (§3.3, S2): per-player batches with `u16` lengths; entity references by `u32` serial.
13. **The PoC is JavaScript (`.mjs`), not TypeScript**: no build step, and it runs in Node and the browser unchanged. Its client runs networking on the simulation's thread, except for the shadow worker. Production code follows 10 §2 and A1.
14. **Player-facing strings are only proposals** (DESIGN §8.4), for 08's owner, as `pm/README.md` requires.

## Review response

Response to `pm/reviews/A2-r1.md`. All 17 findings are **Fixed** in DESIGN.md; none is rejected. For finding 17 the fix is to defer the work the reviewer flagged, as the reviewer proposed. Where the PoC could confirm a fix cheaply, it was patched and tested (DESIGN §12.9); everything else is design-only and listed as such in §12.10.

1. **Critical — restart can fork the action history. Fixed.** DESIGN §6.9 was rewritten.
   - **Write before broadcast:** each closed batch is written to the log with `write(2)` before any client receives it, and flushed every second (or per batch in a durable mode). A process crash therefore loses nothing.
   - **Room generations:** every relay start opens one; clients resume only within it and only up to the persisted horizon. A client that is ahead, which only a machine crash allows, rewinds by re-syncing from the stored checkpoint.
   - **No adoption:** the relay never adopts closures from clients.
   - Also in §0 row 11, §2.6 (resume), §6.4 and §9 (crash-point tests). Design-only.
2. **Major — adaptive stamping can reverse a player's actions. Fixed.**
   - **Rule:** the relay assigns `max(clamp(target, open, open + 600), lastAssigned[player])`, keeps send order within a tick, and clients stamp nondecreasing targets; prediction reconciles against `ACTACK` assignments (§3.4, new D21, S2's debug assertion).
   - **PoC:** relay and client patched. A probe sends start at tick 48 and stop at tick 38: the relay assigns 48 to both, and the closure runs them in send order.
3. **Major — reconnect lacks action identity and input fencing. Fixed.**
   - **Identity:** actions are `(player, u32 seq)` with seq continuous across reconnects; `WELCOME` returns `acceptedThrough`; the relay deduplicates and re-acknowledges; clients re-send only above the watermark (§3.3).
   - **Fencing:** held-input actions carry a per-player input generation that `release_inputs` raises, so a start stamped before a release cannot undo it (§3.3, new D22, S1).
   - **Replacement connections** open at once, without waiting for the old socket's close event, because `close()` drains queued sends first [41] (§2.6).
   - **PoC:** the watchdog now replaces the socket immediately. Watermarks and fencing are design-only.
4. **Major — digest timeout can prevent comparison. Fixed.**
   - **Rule:** verdicts are kept for the 60-s maximum lag and late reports are compared against them; expected membership is defined at the checkpoint tick; epochs are owned by the relay and checked; donors come only from the winning group (§5.1, §5.2).
   - **PoC:** all four implemented. The new `lagreport` scenario has a player 6 s behind corrupt its state: it was caught on the late path, repaired from a winning-group donor, and ended in full-state agreement. The run also exposed and fixed a handover bug (a shadow ahead of a lagging live world never swapped).
5. **Major — snapshot digest lineage missing. Fixed.**
   - **Lineage:** every checkpoint carries `r` at its tick and its epoch, beside the state but outside its hash (§5.1, S7, S9, D8).
   - **No cursor:** slices are now chosen by key (entity serial, chunk and record keys modulo 3,600), so there is no cursor to save.
   - **Full state in tests:** D18 compares full canonical state, not only digests, in join equivalence, NO_SLEEP, heavy mode, cross-engine and the multi-client harness.
   - **PoC:** the runner now requires full-state agreement; all 12 scenarios pass it.
6. **Major — empty-room upgrades replay old actions under new rules. Fixed** (§6.3, §4.6).
   - Upgrades start only from a **sealed** checkpoint (an empty log). An unsealed room is first sealed by replaying its log under the room's own build, loaded from a build archive kept while any stored room references it.
   - The sealed checkpoint is then migrated, and published atomically together with the new build pin. Design-only.
7. **Major — clock transitions underspecified. Fixed** (§3.1, §3.6, S3).
   - **Clock generations:** `CLOCK{gen, fromTick, speed, paused}` messages, with generations on `TICKS`, `ACTACK` and `PONG`. Clients drop old samples, rebase playout and recompute margins.
   - **Pause semantics** are explicit, and relay stalls are rebased instead of bursting.
   - **Pause while joining** closes the marker's tick before pausing. Design-only.
8. **Major — queues and dependency retention unbounded. Fixed** (§6.5). New limits:
   - relay → client control high-water mark 2 MiB, then disconnect;
   - bulk queues hold references, with at most 4 MiB in flight;
   - net worker → simulation queue 64 MiB or 10 minutes, then re-sync;
   - retained log 64 MiB or 2 hours, then a checkpoint and truncation;
   - transfer deadlines;
   - per-room and server-wide storage budgets;
   - blobs kept while any retained closure or stored checkpoint references them (also §3.3).

   Design-only.
9. **Major — wire limits contradict field widths. Fixed** (§3.2, §6.5). Payloads are at most 65,535 bytes; `TICKS` messages hold at most 65,535 entries and about 1 MiB, with ranges split at tick boundaries. Every length, entry tick and range is validated before the horizon moves; malformed input closes the connection (relay) or triggers a resume (client).
   - **PoC:** codec and relay patched. Probes confirm refusal of 65,536-byte payloads and 65,536-entry batches, contiguous splitting of a 70,000-entry range, rejection of truncated `TICKS`, and that a malformed `ACT` closes the connection (code 4002) while the relay survives.
10. **Major — snapshot pacing can stop at low rates. Fixed** (§4.3). The bucket capacity is at least one fragment; fragments carry at most about 25 ms of link time (16 KiB below about 5 Mbit/s); a floor of 16 KiB/s applies; tests run at 2 Mbit/s.
    - **PoC:** both buckets were patched, and paced uploads use 16 KiB chunks. `slowlink-paced` completed a 4.3 MB paced join over 2 Mbit/s in 21.3 s.
    - **Honestly:** the crude pacer still let the donor's RTT reach 85 ms (p50) and 298 ms (p95). The design states a tuned LEDBAT as an implementation requirement, not as solved.
11. **Major — partial-snapshot publication needs a transaction. Fixed** (§4.1, §4.3).
    - **Manifest:** an immutable manifest (tick, room generation, build, protocol, `prototypes.hash`, digest lineage, sizes, chunk size and count, SHA-256).
    - **Chunks:** bounds checked; unique chunks counted; verification before an atomic rename into the room's checkpoint slot; deadlines; the joiner verifies too.
    - **Complete checkpoints:** they now explicitly include cold data, timers, RNG, the blueprint store, per-player state and pending worldgen requests (D8, S9).
    - **PoC:** the relay counts unique, in-bounds chunks; probes confirm that a duplicate does not complete a snapshot and an out-of-range index is ignored. The manifest and SHA-256 are design-only.
12. **Major — position fallback can target a replacement entity. Fixed** (§3.3, S5, new D23).
    - Existing entities are named by serial, and just-predicted ones by creation action id, resolved through a per-player ring of the serials created by the last 256 actions.
    - There is no position fallback; coordinates only for actions defined on positions. Design-only (the toy simulation has no entity references).
13. **Major — no state-replacement protocol for consumers. Fixed** with the new **S15** (M0-mandatory). A state generation on every packet, query response and event, raised on new game, load, join, re-sync swap and rewind. On a new generation:
    - full view invalidation;
    - subscription rebinding, with open entity windows rebinding by serial;
    - rejection of stale responses;
    - the latency state discarded and rebuilt;
    - audio state reset.

    Historical events from catch-up or replay are marked and suppressed (S10, D15). Design-only.
14. **Major — a second worker does not guarantee convergence. Fixed** (§5.2).
    - The shadow's progress is measured: it must gain at least 20% of game speed over 10 s and be projected to converge within 60 s. Otherwise the client switches to foreground repair, or to the slow-machine policy if even that cannot exceed game speed.
    - At most 3 repairs per 10 minutes. Real-engine resource tests under CPU contention are added (§9 item 4).
    - The convergence check is design-only. The PoC's shadow handover fix is noted under finding 4.
15. **Major — evidence overstates completed behavior. Fixed.**
    - §12 was rewritten on a final run of the patched PoC. The runner now requires full canonical-state agreement and reports whether every player got into the game.
    - The new §12.10 states what the PoC does not establish: prediction exactness itself; CPU contention (emulated costs are blocking waits); repairs on weak machines; slow players (who never finished joining); and the design-only parts.
    - §9's ship gates now require completed joins and swaps, post-fault full-state agreement, and real-engine resource measurements. This report's verification section was corrected likewise.
16. **Minor — browser guarantees need qualification. Fixed.**
    - HTTPS working no longer implies WebSocket working (§0 row 4, §2.5).
    - The WebTransport row is corrected: Safari 26.4 does fall back to HTTP/2 over TCP [42]; Chrome and Firefox were verified only over HTTP/3 [24][25][43]. The verdict is unchanged: the automatic QUIC attempt is the risk, and the fallback adds nothing over a WebSocket.
    - Safari's documented background behaviour (timer throttling, iOS suspension, App Nap) is cited [44].
    - A version-specific capability matrix is added (§2.2), background execution is declared best effort everywhere, and release Safari is tested each release (§9 item 8).
17. **Minor — delta repair is presented as cheaper than it is. Fixed by deferral, as proposed** (§5.2, §6.2, §6.7, S9).
    - The text now states that rotating slice hashes cannot provide coherent page hashes at the marker tick, and that a full scan on both sides plus coordination is needed.
    - No page layout is committed in M0, which keeps only canonical section-wise serialization and complete checkpoints. Delta repair and the Cloudflare adapter are deferred until full-snapshot recovery works.
