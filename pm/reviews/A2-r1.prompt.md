You are a senior reviewer of distributed systems and networking for real-time games. Review the multiplayer architecture for the browser game *Paperwork*, a deterministic Factorio-like whose simulation runs at 60 UPS in a Web Worker.

Read:
- `design/multiplayer/DESIGN.md` (the design under review);
- `pm/wp/A2-multiplayer.md` (the brief and the user's constraints: trusted clients; the web server must not run the simulation; NAT traversal must be invisible to players; NO peer-to-peer UDP);
- `pm/reports/A2.md`;
- the proof-of-concept code in `design/multiplayer/poc/`, as needed.

For context, the engine spec is `design/spec/10-architecture.md` (§3, §4, §8).

What to look for:
- **Correctness of the lockstep scheme:** the relay-owned clock, client-stamped intended ticks moved forward by the relay, input delay and latency hiding, pacing and catch-up, join with a snapshot and buffered actions, desync detection and automatic repair, reconnects, rooms without a host.
- **Determinism:** hazards and gaps in the rules M0 must follow.
- **Factual errors** about browser networking: WebRTC ICE-TCP, TURN over TCP/TLS, WebTransport, WebSocket behaviour, hidden-tab timer throttling in Chrome, Firefox and Safari.
- **Failure modes the design misses:** clock drift, a slow client, tab backgrounding, a large join on a slow uplink, a relay restart, an action flood, partial snapshots, version skew between clients.
- **Over-engineering** for a co-op game of two to eight trusted players, and **under-specification** that would block implementation.
- **The engine seams** marked *M0-mandatory*: are they complete and correct, and is any missing?

Output: a prioritized list of findings, at most 1,500 words. Tag each one Critical, Major or Minor, and for each give the file and section, the problem, why it matters, and a concrete fix. End with a three-line overall verdict. Do not modify any files.
