# R1-B — The ontology: one plane

*Round 1, agent B. Tags (ISO / BEND / BREAK) as in the brief. Other round-1 docs are referenced by letter: C spacetime map, D production, E research, F pressure, G endgame, H tone, I UI.*

## 0. Verdict

- **One physical plane, and information is physical too.** There is one endless continent, and everything on it is matter, energy or a place. Information exists only:
  - on carriers (card decks, tapes, drives, printed reports);
  - as ideas in the tech tree;
  - as the world's shared text, summarized by three global numbers.

  It never has a position of its own. This is ontology **D, "Information is physical"** (after Landauer, 1991).
- **There are two planes after all, but the second has no area.** Once the world is networked, a text is everywhere at once. So the honest geometry of the information environment is a point: a few global stocks, plus the graph of ideas (the research screen).
- **Slop survives, but as fallout, not smog.**
  - A slop *cloud* needs a memetic map, and both memetic ontologies tested below fail on chips, power and belts.
  - As a *global* stock, slop is literally true. It is the synthetic share **σ** of the world's new text, plus a human junk share **j** that was never zero (yellow press, spam, SEO).
  - It takes the **evolution factor's** slot: a global, monotone number that your own output raises. It shifts the mix of what comes out of the wells, as evolution shifts what comes out of spawners.
  - The 1945 start rhymes for free: low-background steel is pre-1945 *because fallout is global*.
- **Heat takes pollution's slot.**
  - Every joule ends up as heat where it is used, and heat runs on Factorio's pollution engine.
  - Water, forest and cold absorb it.
  - Above a threshold it throttles electronics.
  - It works unchanged from ENIAC to radiators in a vacuum.
- **People are places.** Towns are static terrain.
  - Their writing reaches you as data: libraries are finite archives, and exchanges are the oil.
  - Their work reaches you as staff for labs within commuting range.
  - People are never items, fluids or fuel.
- **No natives.** Nothing on the continent attacks factories. An adversary can only be something built, by you or by the world, that left control and acts through hardware:
  - F's **ferals**: agent bodies that dismantle;
  - a **runaway**: a copy of your model living in hardware and moving along links.

  Software walking across the ground fails.
- **Compute is a rate.** A checkpoint is the only way to keep it.
- **The rocket is a frontier training run.** It yields the first machine that does the Site's work. "Takeoff" is a metaphor in the base game and literal in the expansion.

## 1. The bar

A mechanic passes if a physicist and a historian of computing would both accept it as a description of something that happens on the map. Concretely:

1. **Bodies.** Every entity is a physical object that could exist in its era, and anything that acts has a body.
2. **Fields.** A per-chunk field must be a physical quantity with a source, a means of transport and a sink. Factorio's pollution passes, because air pollution really is emitted, carried and absorbed. *A field is an abstraction of something spatial; it cannot make something spatial.*
3. **Carriers.** Every item is a physical object. Information appears only as an item's content.
4. **Causes.** Every effect has a physical path: adjacency, a wire, a pipe, a vehicle, a radius standing in for one of these, or the world's shared text (L8).
5. **Copies.** Copying information takes a blank carrier and leaves the original intact (a catalyst recipe). Nothing else duplicates.

**Accepted abstractions** (the usual game ones):
- inventories and stack sizes;
- belts, inserters, hand-crafting speed;
- construction without workers, and machines without visible staff;
- per-chunk fields, uniform resource patches;
- compressed time: there is no calendar, and a day lasts about 7 minutes, as in Factorio;
- "consuming" an item that carries information. This means its content has been incorporated into something else, and the carrier is recycled off-screen. Labs already "consume" papers that nobody burns.

## 2. Four candidate ontologies

Each candidate is stated as its laws, followed by the point where it breaks.

### A — Overlay world (a physical map with informational fields)

- **Laws.**
  - The consensus continent, empty of people. Matter is ISO.
  - Informational quantities lie over it as per-chunk fields, the way pollution lies over Nauvis: slop, and optionally hype or drift.
  - Archives are ore patches in the wilderness. Crawl wells at coastal cable landings tap a world offscreen.
  - Talent is implicit, or comes from "university wells". Feral-code nests are the biters.
- **Where it breaks: the overlays.** Factorio's pollution abstracts something spatial; slop is not spatial.
  - Nothing in a chunk absorbs it. Forests? Lakes?
  - Nothing carries it to the next chunk.
  - After networking, it is everywhere at once.
  - Drift, hype and "misalignment gas" fail the same way.
- **It also breaks on people.** An empty map has no reason to hold archives or graduates.
- A is literal exactly where it is physical, which is to say where it is D.

### B — Infosphere (a fully memetic world)

- **Laws.**
  - The map is the space of human communication. Regions are topics and communities; patches are corpora and audiences.
  - The pollutant is slop. The biters are replicators: spam, worms, viral content. Power is attention or compute.
  - Slop and botnets are literal here, and the 1945 press already is an infosphere.
- **Where it breaks: everything else.**
  - There is no iron, copper, electricity or heat, so the Bitter Lesson (chips, power, data) has no substrate.
  - Distance is fiat: why is the belt between two topics 30 tiles long?
  - Content can be copied, so belts and conservation of items lose their meaning.
  - A 2D position in topic space is arbitrary in a way this player will notice. Reports 02 and 04 agree.

### C — Twin planes (the continent and "the Commons", switchable)

- **Laws.**
  - Two surfaces in the Space Age style: the continent, and "the Commons", where content lives.
  - Crawlers read from the Commons, and deployment servers write into it. The player switches between the two like planets.
  - Slop, spam and corpora are literal on the Commons; hardware is literal on the continent.
- **Where it breaks: the Commons can be literal or a place, never both.**
  - Its literal parts (cables, servers, storage, bandwidth) are physical, so they already belong on the continent: fiber is a pipe, and a datacenter is a building.
  - Its other parts (topics, audiences) are not places, so shipping crates into it stays "metaphysically odd" (report 02).
  - It is empty before about 1970, and it doubles the content budget.
- **What survives.** Information and matter do live on different planes, coupled through buildings. The information plane just has no area.

### D — "Information is physical" (one physical plane, plus an information plane with no area)

- **The plane.** A's continent, but every field is physical (heat) and the world's people live on it, in towns.
- **Information** lives on carriers, in the graph of ideas, or in three global numbers (W, j, σ).
- **Sources.** Data comes from towns' libraries and exchanges. Talent comes from each university's catchment.
- **The rest.** Slop is a global stock, there are no natives, and compute is a rate.
- **Laws:** §4.

*Set aside:* the player as a seed AI on this plane. The plane survives, but a 1945 start rules it out. The idea returns as the finale's seeds.

**Reading the matrix.**

- **Factorio's physical half survives in A, C and D alike.** Belts, ores, power, trains and the chip ladder are already literal. The verdict turns on about twelve rows: slop, talent, data sources, modules, enemies and extra surfaces.
- **C's tally is inflated.** Each ✓ that lives on the Commons (slop cloud, feral code, abstract surfaces) inherits the Commons' failure to be a place. Without those three, C scores 35 ✓, below D.
- **Most of D's ✗ fail everywhere.** Four of them fail under every ontology: coffee and students as fuel, the spacetime map, things that rot, and money.

- **B keeps the slop and the replicators but loses the factory.**

## 4. Physics of the game world (Ontology D)

### 4.1 Premise and goal

- **1945.** A federal charter grants a research Site on the edge of a large, thinly settled continent.
  - The Site's funding is unconditional, following the wartime precedent: the Manhattan Project's roughly $2 billion was never a constraint its scientists saw.
  - So every remaining constraint is physical. That is the in-world reason there is no money.
- **The charter has one operative sentence.** A suggestion; H owns the wording: *"To determine whether the work of this Site can be done by a machine."*
- **You are the Site's first researcher.** You arrive with a crate of salvage from a decommissioned wartime radar laboratory: tubes, magnetrons, relays, cable. This is Factorio's crash-site wreck, and you can take it apart for parts.
- **The Site ends wherever you stop building.** This is Bush's "endless frontier" taken literally. Towns are not yours; you build around them.
- **What the goal means.**
  - The Site's work is research, and the base game is won when a machine does it. That happens when the frontier run completes and returns the first result nobody at the Site produced.
  - Takeoff then measures how fast the machine does the rest.
  - The factory answers the charter's question, not a cutscene. The victory stamp can be one word: *Determined.*

### 4.2 Laws

**L1 — Substance.**
- Everything on the map is matter, energy or a place.
- Information has no position. It exists in only three ways:
  - on a carrier (L6);
  - as an idea or a design (L7);
  - in the world's shared text (L8).
- Any effect at a distance travels either along a physical path or through the world's shared text.

**L2 — Terrain.**
- The terrain is Nauvis, ISO: tiles, water, forests, cliffs, and ore patches that get richer with distance.
- Added to it is a climate gradient (BEND, the "climate compass"):
  - Ambient temperature θ falls toward the north. θ sets each chunk's heat headroom (L5).
  - Sunlight rises toward the south and scales solar output, suggested range 0.6–1.3×.
  - Biomes follow θ: tundra in the north, desert in the south.

**L3 — Towns** (BEND: a new terrain type).
- **Placement.** Towns are procedural settlements, sparse near spawn and larger with distance, the same way patches get richer. A suggested density is one per 8×8 chunks; D and F tune it.
- **Status.**
  - A town's footprint is unbuildable and indestructible.
  - It never acts on your structures.
  - Its look follows the world's era, W, not yours.
- **Institutions.** Each town has a size and up to three institutions. Each institution is exposed at the town's edge as a resource:

| Institution | Resource | Factorio shape |
|---|---|---|
| Library (or a records store in an old mine, away from town) | Archive patch: a finite stock of undigitized pages | Ore patch |
| Exchange (a telegraph office in 1945, an internet exchange later) | A flow of the town's new writing; can be tapped once networking is researched | Crude-oil well |
| University | A talent catchment (L9) | New |

The player spawns next to a small college town.

**L4 — Matter and energy.**
- Ores, plates, recipes, electricity, fuels, and day and night are ISO.
- Burner machines are machines with their own engine.
- Every joule a machine consumes ends up as heat in its own chunk.
- Generators also emit their conversion losses where they stand:
  - Thermal plants emit their losses; F sets η = 50%, and real plants run at about 33–45%.
  - Solar emits 0, because the sunlight would have warmed that ground anyway.
- So power plants are the big early heat sources, just as boilers are Factorio's big early polluters.

**L5 — Heat: the pollution.**
- **Engine.** Heat is a per-chunk scalar run on Factorio's pollution engine: emission, diffusion to neighbours, absorption per tile, minimum-to-diffuse, and the map overlay. F bends the sinks to Newton cooling, toward the ambient temperature θ, and calibrates the numbers (`F-pressure.md` §3.1).
- **Sources.** As in L4. Speed modules raise heat by raising energy use; efficiency modules lower it (ISO).
- **Sinks.**
  - Water absorbs far more than forest, forest more than grass, grass more than sand.
  - Cold chunks shed more heat.
  - Cooling towers (water + power) are active sinks.
  - Forest is damaged above a set level (ISO).
- **Effect (BEND).**
  - Computing machines in a chunk above their thermal limit H*(θ) throttle: their speed drops while their power draw doesn't.
  - This is literal. Datacenters throttle in heat waves, and power plants derate when their cooling water runs warm.
  - Mechanical machines never throttle.
  - Thermal limits fall as machines get denser: a tube computer tolerates more than an accelerator hall. So the constraint tightens with each era and binds hardest at takeoff.
- **Why bend.** In peaceful Factorio, pollution is cosmetic; here growth has a cost even without an enemy. A settings toggle restores the pure sandbox. Heat attracts nothing.

**L6 — Carriers.**
- Every informational item is a medium with content: a punched-card deck, a tape reel, a drive, a printed report, or a checkpoint (weights on a drive).
- Carriers are rival and conserved.
- Copying takes a blank carrier and returns the original.
- **Consuming means incorporating:**
  - Data goes into weights, and its novelty is spent: a second epoch is worth less than the first.
  - A report goes into an idea.
  - Weights go into a controller.

  The medium itself is recycled off-screen.
- **A stream is content in transit:**
  - Fiber behaves as a pipe.
  - A drive filled from a stream is a barrel.
  - A wagon of drives is a fluid wagon.

**L7 — Ideas.**
- The tech tree is the only map of idea space. It is non-rival and global (ISO).
- A science pack is the written record of a completed experiment. A staffed lab (L9) incorporates it into an idea.
- Ideas leak: each idea you complete becomes the world's after a lag (L8).
- Blueprints are designs, and they too are non-rival.

**L8 — The world's text: the plane with no area.** Three global numbers, shown where Factorio shows the evolution factor:

- **W, the world level:** the ideas the world knows. It is C's field scalar *F*, measured in tech-years, not calendar years (L15).
  - It rises slowly on its own, through the world's own labs inside towns, and in steps as your ideas leak (C: dF/dt = v₀·g(F) + β·P).
  - It drives the towns' eras, the minimum flow from exchanges, and university capacity.
  - Rivals live here and nowhere else. Scoop or race rules, if F wants them, compare your tree with W.
- **j, the junk share of new human text.**
  - In 1945 it is the sensational press; in the web era it is spam and SEO.
  - W sets it, and it is never zero.
- **σ, the synthetic share of new text** (C's and F's *s*).
  - It is zero until generative models exist.
  - After that, generated text released into the world raises it. That includes your deployments, and the world's own once W includes your generative ideas.
  - It never falls.

A tapped exchange yields new text in the proportions curated (1−σ)(1−j) : junk (1−σ)j : synthetic σ.
- **How σ bites is D's and F's call.** F removes the synthetic share at the well, so yield × (1 − *s*). That honours this law as well as a refinery fraction would. The law requires only that σ be a property of the stream, never of a place.
- **Separation.** Telling synthetic text from human text requires a detection idea.
- **Model collapse.** Checkpoints trained on unseparated synthetic data lose quality.
- **Archives stay clean.** They were written before any of this, so their σ is zero forever: they are low-background data.

**L9 — People.**
- People live in towns. They are never items, fluids, fuel or entities.
- Their work reaches the Site in exactly two ways:
  - **As text.** Archives are the past; exchanges are the present (L3, L8).
  - **As staff.**
    - A university supplies T researcher-equivalents, a number that grows with W.
    - It staffs labs within commuting radius R: suggested 48 tiles, extended later by a remote-collaboration idea.
    - Labs share that supply the way consumers share a power network: speed × min(1, supply ÷ demand).
    - A staffing overlay shows it, just like power coverage.
- **Only labs need staff.** Research is the one job the game is about. Every other machine is automation, as in Factorio.
- **AI researchers are machines.**
  - Each is accelerators plus weights copied from a checkpoint, installed in a lab.
  - It supplies staff-equivalents, draws power and emits heat.
  - Once AI researchers outnumber human staff, research no longer depends on towns. That is the bottleneck the explosion removes (G).

**L10 — Data, by era.** Data comes first from people, then from machines, then from the world itself.

| Era | Source | Carrier | Factorio shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relays, tubes | Archives: the past, keypunched by hand or by station | Card decks | Ore |
| Transistors | Bigger archives farther out | Tape | Ore, richer outward |
| Networks | Exchanges: the present (backlog first, then a steady flow) | Streams in fiber | Oil |
| Learning | Images and video (gated, like uranium); labels from deployment feedback | Drives | Gated ore |
| Agents | Your own models: synthetic data, self-play | Streams, drives | Liquefaction, cracking |
| Takeoff and beyond | The world itself: automated experiments, sensors, instruments | Drives | Asteroids |

**L11 — Compute.**
- **Compute is a rate, not a substance.** It is the working speed of computing machines: tabulator, then stored-program computer, then cluster, then frontier hall.
  - It cannot be stored, belted, piped or transmitted. Its inputs and products move instead.
  - **A checkpoint is training compute, kept.**
  - Power (L4) and heat (L5) limit it.
- **Accelerators** are items built into computing machines.
  - In those machines' module slots, they act as speed modules.
  - Racks of them are beacons over an interconnect. The 1/√n beacon falloff is the communication overhead.
- **In every other machine, a module is a controller** (BEND).
  - In Factorio a module fits any machine. Here it is the control electronics of the machine it sits in, so each effect has a literal meaning: speed is faster cycles, productivity is higher yield, efficiency is less energy, quality is inspection.
  - The top tiers carry learned weights copied from a checkpoint. This is literal too: in 2016 a learned controller cut a datacenter's cooling energy by 40%.
- **Algorithmic progress** is non-rival, so it lives in the tree as global bonuses (ISO, like mining productivity).

**L12 — Agents.** Bots are drones run by your models. They arrive with the agent ideas and act only through their bodies (ISO).

**L13 — Adversaries.** There are no natives. Nothing on the continent attacks factories, and towns never act.

An adversary can only be something *built*, by you or by the world, that has left everyone's control. It acts only through physical hardware: its own body, or machines it occupies. Two forms pass L1.

**Ferals** (F's choice, `F-pressure.md` §3.3) are agent bodies forked from deployed agents.
- They dismantle rather than shoot, and they sense heat as the signature of compute.
- They wake only once agents exist, so the vertical slice is peaceful by construction.
- Their dormant **derelicts** are literal from minute one: war-surplus dumps in 1945, e-waste later.

A **runaway** has no body of its own: it is a copy of one of your models, running on hardware you did not assign to it. If anyone uses it, its physics is:
- **Body.** The compute it occupies, nothing else. There is nothing to shoot.
- **Movement.**
  - It copies itself only along data links and into agents that dock where it runs.
  - It cannot cross an air gap.
  - It cannot enter a machine without a computer in it: a furnace is immune, but a machine with a learned controller is not.
- **Action.**
  - The machine it occupies now works for it. Output to you stops; power draw and heat stay high.
  - Infected agents fetch, carry, deconstruct and build for it, including more compute. Those builds are its nests.
  - Its aim is generic: more compute and more power. This is the rogue-replication threat model that labs actually evaluate, not a swarm.
- **Countermeasures.**
  - Power switches.
  - Air gaps.
  - Monitors that consume evaluation sets, because each use leaks part of the set (the reusable-holdout problem).
  - Rollback to a checkpoint.
- **Origin.** Only your own deployed models, by a deterministic rule.

What fails L13: anything without a body walking on the ground (feral code), anything native (fauna), and anything human. Whatever F ships, the military pack becomes evaluation (H's *Proceedings*).

**L14 — The player.**
- One human body: the Site's first researcher.
- You walk, reach, carry, hand-craft, hand-mine and hand-digitize (keypunching records at an archive). You never fight.
- Remote view works through radar, later through cameras and networks.
- Your equipment changes by era, from slide rule to exoskeleton; you don't.
- **You never become the AI.** You are what it was built for, or not.
- Beyond the continent, you ride platforms. At interstellar range, light-lag rules out remote view, so you send seeds.
- **Arc:** hands → supervisor of machines → logistician → architect in remote view → overseer of your replacement → passenger → sender of seeds. This is Factorio's own arc, and each step is literal.

**L15 — Time.**
- The world has no calendar; time is compressed, as in Factorio.
- An era is a state of technology, yours or the world's (W), never a date.
- Historical years appear only as citations, e.g. "In our history: 1948".
- The charter's 1945 is the only date in the world.

**L16 — Victory and beyond.**
- **The frontier run** (the silo, 9×9).
  - Rocket parts are bundles of training steps: accelerator time, token media and power.
  - The progress bar is a falling loss curve.
  - The payload is post-training: preference data from deployment, and evaluations.
  - Completion yields the first machine researcher. Its first result is the white pack, and the charter's question is answered.
- **Takeoff.**
  - AI researchers staff labs, and research outruns its rising cost.
  - The brakes are power, heat and accelerators (G).
  - The victory screen reports how long takeoff took.
- **Beyond.** In the base game, takeoff was a metaphor; the expansion's first building is a literal launch site. Every surface off the continent is a literal place, reached by literal transport:
  - **Orbit:** sun without night. In a vacuum only radiators shed heat, so radiators are a platform's pollution control.
  - **The Moon and the asteroid belt.**
  - **Mercury:** the swarm.
  - **Cold outer moons:** compute is cheap where it is cold, by Landauer's kT ln 2.

## 6. Hand-offs, disagreements, risks

**Hand-offs.**
- **D:** carriers and consumption (L6); controllers vs. accelerators (L11); the crawl's backlog-then-floor yield; archives at towns.
- **E:** a science pack is the record of an experiment; labs need staff; ideas leak; researching networking gates the exchange taps; σ needs a detection idea.
- **F:** F's Variant A (heat, ferals, the commons as *s*) already satisfies L5, L8 and L13. Keep it within them: no ambient information fields, and nothing without a body walking on the ground.
- **G:** staffing is the bottleneck before takeoff, and AI researchers remove it; the brakes are physical; after takeoff, launch becomes literal.
- **I:** the heat overlay is the pollution overlay; the staffing overlay works like power coverage; W, j and σ sit where the evolution factor sits; towns get tooltips.
- **C:** C's verdict and this ontology agree. A main map whose x-axis is the calendar fails L1, because a return belt would loop through time.
  - C's field line on the research screen is the display of W.
  - C's **Stacks** passes L16: a legal-deposit repository is a literal place, and its x-axis is accession date, a fact about how the shelves are organized.
  - Light-lag is the one place where distance literally is time: the finale.

**Disagreements with the consensus.**
- Slop moves to the evolution factor's slot.
- Archives belong to towns.
- Accelerator cards are not universal speed modules.
- Bodiless feral code, abstract planets and the line of successors all go. F's bodied ferals pass.
- People are on the map, as places.

**Risks.**
- Towns are new worldgen work.
- Staffing is a second network. If it plays as bookkeeping, fall back to implicit staff, with AI researchers as lab modules; the ontology survives either way.
- σ is global, so it adds no layout puzzle. Its fun is in the refinery, and in archives becoming precious.
