# R1-C — The spacetime map: where a time axis is literally true, and where it is not

Task: take Concept 3 ("Frontier", `brainstorm/04`, the x-axis is the calendar and a frontier sweeps east) seriously; find the readings that are literally true inside the world; spec the strongest; give a verdict a Factorio veteran accepts.

**Short answer.** On a physical map, "x = year" is a label, and the user's objection is exact: either nothing is really dated (then why force the direction?) or things are (then walking west is time travel and every return belt loops through time). Nothing in the ground has a date; transistor patches are fiat. But two things in this game *are* literally dated: **data** (text written in year Y cannot exist before Y, the stock grows at the world's writing rate, and "the present" is its edge) and **the field** (real techs have years; the field advances whether or not you do). Those are where the wildcard lives. Kill the main-map version; build the field's frontier line on the research screen (cheap, base game) and one expansion surface, **the Stacks**, whose x-axis is accession date and whose moving edge is the Present (the one literal spacetime *map*, and a fully specified planet). The one place a frontier line literally sweeps across physical space is the interstellar finale, and Concept 3's dynamical win condition belongs there.

## 1. The test

A reading passes only if all three hold:

1. **Literal.** "x = time" must be a fact about the world the player stands in. Concretely: an item moving west must mean nothing odd.
2. **Factorio's map jobs hold by physics**, not fiat (02 §1): patches, distance costing infrastructure, depletion, texture, the avatar's locality fading.
3. **Pulled, not pushed.** Factorio's expansion is pulled by depletion and richness and the direction is the player's choice. A line that moves whether or not you do is a treadmill, *unless* the thing being left behind is supposed to be left behind: a mine.

## 2. The readings

| # | Reading | What x literally is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Spacetime diagram, light cones | *When.* | Dead for a factory. In a spacetime diagram every belt is a worldline, nothing can loop (Kovarex, sushi, return lines), and a persistent factory is a horizontal band drawn once: you would watch it, not build it. The only place a light-cone frontier literally sweeps across a map is 03's interstellar finale (the grabby front advances at a fraction of *c*). Concept 3's win rule, *build faster than the frontier moves*, is that finale's territory race and should move there (§6). |
| R2 | "The future is already here, not evenly distributed" | Development level. | Already Factorio. The lab is the most advanced point; the sweep is your own technology diffusing outward, which is the base growing. The reversed version (the lab in a backwater chasing the developed world eastward) is nonsense: you cannot walk to catch up. |
| R3 | The adjacent possible, the frontier of the feasible | What can be built given what exists. | This *is* the tech tree: researched = settled, available = the frontier, locked = beyond. As terrain it duplicates the research screen with worse legibility. Factorio already gates *use* without gating *existence* (uranium is visible from minute one). |
| R4 | Isochrones: Turner's settlement frontier, Dutch polders, the Oklahoma land runs, glacial retreat | *When the frontier passed here.* | Literally true wherever a real process sweeps and changes the land. It can explain *access* (survey, charter, rail) but never era-stratified *hardware*: transistors are not in the ground in 1955 and absent in 1950. What survives is a release schedule (§3). |
| R5 | History as strata you build on | Age of what stands here. | Your base already is this; every veteran knows the walk from the starter spaghetti to the city blocks is a walk through time. Make it visible for free (§6). |
| R6 | **Dated data** | Date of the material. | The one resource with a literal date. A chronologically shelved repository is a spacetime map by construction; its moving edge is the present. Build it as a surface (§4). |
| R7 | **The field's frontier** | The field's year. | Real techs are dated; your position is dated by your newest tech; the field moves on its own and faster when you publish. The research screen is already a map (nodes, edges, an available-frontier); the field's line makes it spacetime (§5). |

Concept 3 as written is R4's geometry carrying R3's content on a physical map, and that combination is exactly where the time-travel nonsense comes from.

## 3. Why it dies as the main map

The objections, in the order a veteran would raise them:

1. **The ontology is false.** A chalk patch "at 1950" is running in the present: you can see its drills turn. If x is only a label, the forced direction has no justification; if x is a date, then hauling chalk east is fine but every return belt, every train going back for more, and the player walking home are time travel. Principle 2 (one plane) fails on the first belt loop.
2. **Pushed, not pulled.** Factorio's rhythm is *patch depletes → choose where next*. Here the direction is fixed and the pace is scripted. The one choice Factorio's map gives you most often is removed.
3. **The forced bus.** "Red science forever means chalk hauled from 1950 to the present" sounds romantic and is a tail that lengthens at the frontier's speed forever. Factorio's long bus is a choice you can refactor; this one cannot be refactored, only extended. The fix (let old resources appear behind the frontier too) deletes the timeline-bus aesthetic and leaves a map with a forced direction for no reason.
4. **The promised feeling dies when it should peak.** "Feel acceleration with your feet" is measured in walking, and walking is the thing trains, bots and remote view delete by the blue tier. The singularity would arrive as a number in map view, which is what every other concept already offers.
5. **A scripted clock in place of an emergent system.** Pollution-driven evolution is the player's own doing; a frontier that advances at a tile a minute is a timer with scenery.
6. **Dated patches are fiat** (R4 above), and a thin y-axis ("theory to hardware") is a second label with no physics behind it.

**The optional mode.** The only coherent main-map variant is a *release schedule*, "Charter": the reservation opens in dated tracts on a timer, accelerated by papers consumed (Bush's thesis made literal: public science opens the frontier; the land-run image is Turner's). It is the opposite of a treadmill: it holds you back rather than pushing you, like an island preset. Harmless, minor, not the wildcard, not for v1.

## 4. The Stacks (an expansion surface, Fulgora's slot)

Report 01 reserved Fulgora's slot for "the archive and chip design". This fills it with the time axis, and it is the strongest reading because every element is a fact about repositories rather than a metaphor.

### 4.1 Premise and plane

**The Stacks** is a legal-deposit repository on the same physical world: the place every published work on the continent is required to arrive at (legal deposit is a real institution, and the pun on *ore deposit* never needs saying). It is shelved in accession order. Walking east is walking toward more recent material because that is how the building is organized. Nothing is in the past; everything is on a shelf now. The newest shelf is **the Present**: the loading dock where this month's material is being shelved. East of it are prepared, empty halls.

What this surface answers: after your models are deployed (yellow tier, 03 §2.5), the home map's crawl and the open web carry a rising synthetic fraction. Pre-deployment material is the low-background steel of data, and the one place it exists in bulk, dated and sorted, is here. The surface's *second* job is the endgame's (4.8).

### 4.2 Terrain (BREAK, confined to one surface, like each Space Age planet's twist)

- A long hall, endless east, about 400 tiles north–south. Cosmetic strata west to east: shelving, tape vaults, disk arrays, server halls, the dock, then lit empty halls.
- x is divided into **shelves** of 16 tiles, one per field-year (the scalar *F* of §5). Each shelf carries a **band** by medium: *Paper* (oldest), *Tape*, *Disk*, *Web*, *Stream* (the Present's band). Years appear only in tooltips (D6 middle ground).
- y is **collection**: text north, code centre, images south, with soft boundaries. It biases the sorting odds (4.4) without fixing them, so siting matters on both axes.
- A shelf's richness follows Factorio's richness-by-distance curve along x, capped at ×64 of the first shelf: the public text stock roughly doubled per decade, and the cap is "peak data". The newest shelves are the richest and the dirtiest.
- **The Present** advances 16 tiles per field-year: at the base field speed of §5 that is about 0.5 tiles/min, rising to 2–3 tiles/min in the field's own takeoff. A new shelf is deposited with the band and synthetic fraction *current at deposition*; the strata are literally F's history.
- The empty halls east of the Present are **buildable**. You can lay a scanner line 30 tiles ahead and let the Present arrive under it: the factory stands still and the resource comes to it, which is how a crawler works in reality.

### 4.3 Mining (ISO)

**Scanners** (mining drills, 3×3; burner and electric variants exist but only the electric one is worth shipping) mine **Uncatalogued records** from any shelf. Shelves deplete like ore. The front shelf alone yields **Fresh records** instead; the entity type flips to Uncatalogued once the Present has moved a shelf on (BEND: a resource that changes type once, by a simple rule).

### 4.4 Sorting (ISO with Fulgora's recycler)

The **Cataloguer** (Fulgora's recycler: 1 input, 0.2 s, probabilistic outputs) turns 1 Uncatalogued record into one of:

| Band | Text | Code | Image | Labels | Junk | Synthetic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | 60% | 0% | 5% | 5% | 30% | 0% |
| Tape | 50% | 10% | 5% | 5% | 30% | 0% |
| Disk | 40% | 15% | 15% | 5% | 25% | 0% |
| Web | 30% | 20% | 25% | 5% | 20% | 0% |
| Stream | (1 − s) × [25 / 15 / 20 / 5 / 10]% | | | | | **s** |

Collection (y) shifts up to 15 points between text, code and image. Junk is voided or (after *Error correction*) re-sorted at a 50% recovery; Factorio players already void Fulgora's surplus. Labels are the sulfur of this chain: scarce, needed for benchmark recipes, never abundant. Typed outputs stack into **cartridges** (text/code/image), the base game's data items, so everything downstream is the existing curation chain (D's economy).

**s** is the synthetic fraction stored on the shelf at deposition: *s* = min(0.9, 0.1 × (model generations deployed by you and by the field since the deployment era)). Synthetic output is not useless: it recycles into *Distilled text* (fit only for lower-tier packs), or it is voided. *Synthetic detection* (a tech) adds a classifier that routes Synthetic to its own output with a 10% false-positive rate on real text, lowered by an infinite tech: the number every AI-text detector is judged by, stated as a number.

Fulgora's quality loop transfers unchanged: quality modules in Cataloguers plus re-cataloguing surplus is deduplication and filtering, i.e. **data quality is literally quality**, which is the consensus mapping (best-of-N with a verifier) applied to data.

### 4.5 Held-out sets, and the straight answer to "do we time-travel?"

Fresh records, catalogued, yield 10% **Held-out set** in place of part of the text share. A held-out set is a benchmark drawn from material newer than anything a trained model can have seen. It **spoils** (Gleba's mechanic, unchanged) in exactly the time the Present takes to cross one shelf: once that shelf is in the mineable past, the material is training data somewhere, and the set is contaminated. A spoiled set becomes a *Saturated set*, which re-catalogues into text.

This is the only time travel in the game, and it is the real one: a model evaluated on data from after its cutoff has, in the field's own vocabulary, time-travelled, and the mechanic says so by rotting the benchmark. The spoil timer forces just-in-time logistics on one item line, which is what 03 wanted from "scooped" without touching papers.

### 4.6 Pack and techs (hooks for R1-E)

- **Provenance pack** (the surface's science, teal): 1 Held-out set + 1 Curated corpus (any older band) + 1 Drive → 1 pack, 10 s. The corpus must come from a band strictly older than the set, which is a recipe constraint on a per-item band attribute (it uses the quality machinery: band is an ordered attribute, like quality, that filter inserters can read).
- Techs on it: *Error correction*, *Synthetic detection*, *Deduplication* (Cataloguer productivity), *Temporal split* (unlocks the top two quality tiers' verifier, 4.8), infinite *Detector calibration* (false-positive rate 10% → 1%).
- Exportable building: the Cataloguer itself, which is the game's recycler everywhere (Space Age unlocks the recycler on Fulgora the same way).

### 4.7 Power and logistics

- **Mains** (BEND, this surface only): the repository is a building and has a grid connection. Substations at fixed points along the trunk supply a fixed 20 MW each, one per ten shelves, the fixed-yield-well shape of D3's universities. No water, no sun, no fuel on site; you can ship fuel in for generators, but the intended puzzle is rationing. Aquilo's "power is the constraint" is the model.
- **The trunk**: an east–west rail spine is the first thing a player builds, with a station per shelf group. Trains are the decade-spanning logistics tier exactly as Concept 3 wanted, but here because repositories are long, not because the map is time. Return trains are empty trains, not time machines.
- Exports to the home surface are drive wagons (the station wagon full of tapes); imports are accelerators and racks. Which inter-surface transport the expansion layer uses (rocket and platform in Space Age) is the integrator's call; the Stacks takes whatever it is.

### 4.8 Minute to minute, hour to hour

*First ten minutes.* Land at the west end among Paper shelves: thin, clean, 60% text. Hand-mine a few records, hand-catalogue (the hand recipe is slow OCR), place the first scanners and Cataloguers on a Tape shelf, lay the first stretch of trunk east. The Present is visible as a lit dock several hundred tiles away.

*Hour one to three.* The sorting complex: scanner rows, Cataloguer banks, filter inserters by output, junk to the void, cartridges to a train station, the first Provenance packs. Decide where to mine along y (collection) and x (clean-but-thin vs rich-but-dirty). Lay a scanner line *ahead* of the Present so Fresh records flow as the dock arrives; route held-out sets by express train so they reach the harness before they spoil.

*Hours three onward.* Shelves deplete; new scanner lines go east on a predictable axis, which is Factorio's patch-hopping with the direction known in advance. Quality-upcycle corpora. Watch *s* rise at the Present as your own deployments at home grow: the model-collapse story drawn as a stripe on the floor, uncommented. Research *Temporal split* and ship held-out sets home for the verifier.

**Treadmill answer.** A mine is supposed to move. Fulgora players island-hop; here they shelf-hop, with one axis instead of two and a time dimension in exchange: *which vintage* is the decision. The stationary investments (sorting complex, trunk, the home link) are never abandoned; scanner lines are cheap and expected to move. Over-mining catches the Present and leaves you mining only the freshest, dirtiest band: the data wall, as a position on a floor.

### 4.9 The endgame role (why this surface exists at all)

The RSI loop (G's domain) needs a verifier, and a verifier needs an uncontaminated benchmark. Once your models have written half the web, the only uncontaminated material is what the world wrote after your last cutoff: the front shelf. Rule (hook for R1-G/D): *the top two quality tiers' recycler/verifier consumes one fresh held-out set per N items*. The Present advances at the world's writing speed, which does not accelerate with your RSI. So while power and heat brake raw research speed (03 §3.2), **the Present brakes the quality loop**: you cannot verify faster than the world produces things you have not trained on, unless you make your own test material (self-play, which the Stream band's Synthetic output half-permits and half-poisons). That verification, not generation, is the late bottleneck is a correct and unfashionable claim about takeoff, and it arrives as a rate limit rather than a sentence.

### 4.10 Tags

Terrain and the Present: BREAK (one surface; Space Age gives each planet exactly one such twist). Scanners, Cataloguer, quality loop, trunk rail, spoilage: ISO. Fresh-record type flip, Mains, band attribute: BEND. Fun bought: Fulgora's sorting puzzle with a second axis of decision (vintage), a moving resource that rewards building ahead, a spoiling line with a true reason, and a late-game brake nothing else provides.

## 5. The field's frontier line (research screen, base game)

Factorio's research screen is already a map: nodes, edges, and a frontier of available techs ordered left to right by tier. Add one dated vertical line and it is a spacetime map, and this is the home of the one feeling Concept 3 was after.

**The scalar.** *F*, field-years since 1945. Each tech carries a historical year (tooltip only; the DAG is already in year order). Your year *Y* is the year of your newest researched tech. The screen shows the field's dashed line at *F*, your line at *Y*, and a readout: **"7 years ahead."**

**Speed.** dF/dt = v₀ · g(F) + β · P, where *P* is papers consumed per minute across all labs and *g* is the field's own acceleration (1 until the field's year reaches the automated-research era, then hyperbolic with a tunable horizon). Starting numbers: v₀ = 1 field-year per 30 min, so the field needs about 40 hours to cross 80 years, roughly a veteran's time to the rocket; β chosen so that half your research leaks (a paper advances the field half as far as it advances you). *Diffusion is unavoidable*: you cannot research without the field learning from you, and secrecy is not a toggle.

**What it does (BEND, small):**
1. *F* **is** the evolution factor's time term, made visible: pressure phases (03 §3.1) trigger on the field's era as well as your own tier. Nests wake when the field reaches networking whether or not you did.
2. It sets *s* at the Present (4.4) through the field's deployed generations.
3. Default mode: nothing else. No rubber-banding, no discount on techs the field has found (a tech that gets cheaper if you wait is a tech you wait for, which is anti-Factorio), no loss.
4. **Race mode** (03 §2.4, opt-in): if *F* > *Y* for ten minutes after automated research exists, the field took off first; you lose. Only here is the lead a stake.

**Why this is the feeling.** Before the loop closes the line creeps and you are comfortably ahead. In the last stretch your own output drags the field toward you (β·P grows with your purple and yellow throughput). When the loop closes, techs fall in minutes, but *g(F)* is also climbing: two curves on one screen, and your overhang decides whether yours pulls away. The victory screen reports the lead at the end; "FOOM" stays an achievement for a sub-ten-minute takeoff. The acceleration is felt where the RSI is felt anyway, in the UI (05 §3), which no amount of walking could deliver.

Tags: ISO (a display of the time term), BEND (external pacing of pressure phases, diffusion term), BREAK (race mode, opt-in). Hooks: R1-E assigns years; R1-G tunes v₀, β, g.

## 6. Small salvages

- **Age and builder overlay** (ISO shape: an overlay like pollution's). Each entity stores its placement tick and builder (you, or a construction agent). Toggle: colour by age (your base's isochrones) or by builder. In the endgame the ring of agent-built factory around your hand-built core is the frontier, and the credit condition of 04 ("the factory doubles without player input") becomes something you can see from map view. Free; strictly true. For R1-I.
- **Era-skinned tiers make the base strata for free.** Belts, assemblers and poles skinned by era (01) mean a base built outward over forty hours *looks* like 1950 in the middle and 2025 at the rim, with no rule. R5 satisfied.
- **Concept 3's win condition belongs to the finale.** "Win when agents build faster than the frontier moves" is the grabby-frontier territory race of 03 §3.5, where the sweeping line is physics (light-lag, expansion fronts). Keep the rule, move the venue.
- **The training run as a platform trip** (a lead, untested, for whoever specs the expansion layer). Space Age's platform route is the one 1D map Factorio already has where position is time. A frontier training run is its natural reskin: x is training steps, hazards are loss spikes and hardware faults (Llama 3's 419 interruptions), the on-board factory is checkpointing and data loading, and arrival is convergence. Not part of this verdict.

## 7. Verdict

**Kill** the spacetime map as the main map, as a main-map mode, and in its light-cone reading for the base game. Reasons a veteran accepts: the ontology fails on the first return belt; expansion becomes pushed and directionless at once; the bus becomes an unrefactorable tail; the promised feeling is measured in walking, which the game spends its middle act deleting; and dated patches are fiat. The "Charter" release schedule is the only coherent residue and is not worth v1.

**Build, base game:** the field's frontier line on the research screen (§5): one scalar, one dashed line, a "years ahead" readout, *F* as the evolution factor's visible time term with a diffusion term fed by papers consumed, race mode opt-in. This is the transplant 04 already recommended, with the display that makes it felt.

**Build, expansion layer:** the Stacks (§4) in Fulgora's slot. A legal-deposit repository shelved in accession order; the Present as a moving dock; shelves with bands and a stored synthetic fraction; Scanners, the Cataloguer, Fulgora's quality loop as data cleaning; held-out sets mined only at the Present and spoiling as the Present moves on; Mains power; the Provenance pack. It is the only version of "x = time" that is literally true, it answers "do we time-travel?" with the one mechanic where the field itself uses the word, and it gives the RSI endgame the brake on verification that nothing on the home map can provide.

**Relocate:** Concept 3's dynamical win condition to the interstellar finale. **Add free:** the age/builder overlay.

What the main map keeps from all this: nothing. It stays the physical, endless, undated continent of the consensus, and that is the point: time belongs to the things that are actually dated.
