## 0. How to read this

- **Baseline.** I is normative: every rule, screen, number and acceptance test in I applies unless a section here amends it. Nothing in I is repeated here; it is cited.
- **Other round-1 documents** (A–H) are cited for provenance only. Whatever an implementer needs from them is restated here, in 00–06, 08–10 or the data. The icon and sprite rules, D §12 in round 1, are §23.
- **Tags** as in the brief: ISO, BEND, BREAK. §25 collects ours.
- **Priorities** are I's (P0 core loop, P1 veteran-complete, P2 mid-game, P3 late, dev). **[S]** marks what ships in the vertical slice (`09-vertical-slice.md`).
- **Display names** come from `08-text.md` §10 and the data. In particular, Factorio's *Factoriopedia* is displayed as **Handbook**; control IDs keep Factorio's names (`open-factoriopedia`).
- **Who owns what.** The simulation rule behind each readout belongs to 01, 03 and 04 (world, pressure, endgame) or to the data. The UI must draw exactly the predicate the simulation uses: an overlay that says "staffed" for a Seminar the simulation does not staff is a bug, not a styling issue.
- **Numbers on screen.** Percentages round half up. The simulation keeps the exact value, and every surface that shows one quantity shows the same rounded number (impl U7). **One precision table** (R5-gate D2) holds for every quantity shown on more than one surface:

| Quantity | Precision | Where, for example |
|---|---|---|
| Shares and percentages (junk, synthetic and crawl share, satisfaction, yield, speed; not the Leak, which is a count with two decimals) | An integer %; one decimal below 10% | `Synthetic 3.1%` on the strip and `Synthetic share 3.1%` on a Trainer row; `31%` on both once it passes 10% |
| Chunk temperature | One decimal in the chunk tooltip (§3.2) and on the ground-hover line (§7.1), which alt-mode also shows over an entity (§8.1); an integer in entity lines | `Temperature 37.2 °C` and `Chunk 37.2 °C`; `Chunk 37 °C · limit 35 °C · speed 94%` |
| Ambient temperature | As chunk temperature: one decimal in the chunk tooltip and on the ground-hover line | `Ambient 14.0 °C` |
| Durations | §5.1's format: `H:MM:SS` from one hour, `M:SS` from one second, `0.99 s` below one second | `Next copy: 0:42`, `Doubling time 1:10` |
| Above 100 °C | `100+ °C` wherever a chunk or ambient temperature shows (§3.2) | — |
- **Rendering split** as I §8.1: world-anchored overlays in WebGL; windows, tooltips and strips in the DOM; graphs in Canvas2D.

### 3.2 Heat overlay

**What it shows.** The chunk temperature T = θ + H / C of 03 §1.2 (θ the ambient temperature from the climate compass, H the chunk's heat, C its heat capacity). Absolute temperature, never "pollution amount", so ambient is visible before anything is built: the overlay doubles as the siting map.

**Colour scale** (one scale for the map, the minimap and the world tint):

| T | ≤ −15 °C | 0 °C | 20 °C | 35 °C | 45 °C | 70 °C | ≥ 100 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colour | deep blue | blue | white, transparent | orange | red | dark magenta | near-black magenta |

- The legend's last label reads "100+", and every displayed temperature above 100 °C reads "100+ °C" (chunk tooltip, entity lines, ground tile): no mechanic changes above it (03 §1.6). A compact fission block's turbine chunks reach about 430 °C; the scale saturates rather than compressing everything below.
- Alpha rises with |T − 20 °C|, capped at 0.45, so a temperate unbuilt map reads clear and the climate gradient reads as a faint blue-to-orange wash.
- Blue–white–red is legible under the three colour-filter modes of I §4.20; the 45 °C anchor also gets a thin contour line, so it never depends on hue alone.
- **Rendering:** one value per chunk, bilinearly interpolated between chunk centres (Factorio's pollution cloud does the same). Update when the simulation publishes the field (every 64 ticks), never per frame.

**Map (remote view).** Toggle `heat`. Hovering a chunk shows a tooltip:

```
Chunk (12, −4)
Temperature        37.2 °C
Ambient            14.0 °C
Heat in             4.20 MW     (sources in this chunk)
Heat out            3.90 MW     (cooling, diffusion, coolers)
```

A legend strip (the colour scale with tick labels) sits under the toggle grid while the toggle is on.

**Minimap.** In alt-mode the minimap shows the heat layer, exactly where Factorio shows pollution (I §3.14). ISO.

**World view, contextual (BEND, information only).** While the cursor holds or hovers a computing machine, a generator, a cooling building or a machine with coolant ports:
- the chunks in view take the colour scale at alpha ≤ 0.25, under entities;
- the hovered or held machine's **thermal limit** is drawn as the contour where T = T\*_eff (03 §1.3: T\* plus 15 K × its liquid-cooling fraction), labelled "35 °C";
- a cooling tower or dry cooler outlines the chunk it cools; a water exchanger outlines the chunk of the water tile at its pumping position (03 §1.4).

**Which chunk.** An entity's heat, throttle and cooling all use the chunk of its geometric centre; an even-sized entity centred on a chunk line belongs to the chunk east or south of the line (03 §1.1). The outlines and every "Chunk" line use the same chunk.

Factorio draws pollution only on the map; the contextual tint is the one addition, justified because heat binds per chunk and the chunk grid is otherwise invisible in the game view.

**Entity readouts** are in §8. **Heat off** (map setting, 03 §1.9): the `heat` toggle and the minimap layer show ambient θ only, as a siting map for solar and biomes; no Heat statistics tab, no heat lines in tooltips, no throttling statuses.

### 3.3 Staffing overlay

B L9, DECISIONS §7 and 01 §8: a university's coverage is its 8 × 8 campus expanded by R tiles on every side (Chebyshev, like a pole's supply area; R = 48, ×1.5 after *Packet switching* and again after *Web search*, from the data's `staffing-radius` effects). A Seminar is covered when its centre is inside. A working Seminar demands staff in proportion to the speed its modules and Switches give it; lab-speed research is free (01 §8.3). A staffing network shares its supply like a power network. The overlay copies Factorio's power-coverage idiom.

**Geometry** (01 §8.2, impl U3). A campus occupies tiles [x₀, x₀ + 8) × [y₀, y₀ + 8). A Seminar whose centre is (cₓ, c_y) is covered iff x₀ − R ≤ cₓ < x₀ + 8 + R and y₀ − R ≤ c_y < y₀ + 8 + R. A Seminar is 3 × 3, so its centre is always a half-integer and never lies on a coverage edge. The overlay draws exactly these squares.

**Hues** (impl J24). Each network takes hue index = (the lowest id among its universities: the town cell key, 10 §5.10) mod 8 from a fixed palette of eight parchment-to-amber hues, none of them power's blue: `#d9c79a`, `#e0a85a`, `#c9905f`, `#b8a07a`, `#e6c36a`, `#a88a5a`, `#d4b07c`, `#c27f4a`. A network keeps its hue as Seminars come and go; when networks merge, the merged one takes the lowest serial's hue.

**World contextual (P0 [S]).**
- **Holding a Seminar, a Researcher or a Terminal:** every campus's coverage square is tinted at alpha 0.2 in its network's hue, with the campus outlined. The overlay turns on by itself while a Seminar is in the cursor (01 §8.7). The held Seminar's preview shows the **No staff** icon when its centre is outside every coverage square, the way an electric entity shows "not plugged in" once built.
- **Hovering a campus:** its coverage edge is drawn and every Seminar it staffs is highlighted (the pole-network highlight, I §3.1). Tooltip in §7.
- **Hovering a Seminar:** the campus or campuses staffing it are highlighted, and any Terminal that links them.
- **Terminals** (01 §8.9; not in the slice): a line in the network's hue joins every pair of linked Terminals on the same data-line network, as Factorio draws pole wires.

**World warning icons (P0 [S], ISO pattern).** Always visible, like Factorio's power icons: **No staff** (red, a Seminar outside every coverage square) and **Understaffed** (yellow, its network's supply below its demand). Blinking rules are Factorio's.

**World alt-mode (P1).** A Seminar staffed by a Researcher shows the Researcher icon where alt-mode puts module icons.

**Map toggle `staffing` (P1 [S]).** Coverage squares in their network's hue, campuses with their names, Terminal links, and Seminars coloured green (fully staffed), yellow (understaffed) and red (no staff), with a hexagon marker on Researcher-staffed ones (§23: the hexagon means weights).

## 4. The World panel (the evolution factor's slot)

**ISO baseline.** Factorio has no HUD readout for evolution. It prints it with `/evolution` (per surface, with its time, pollution and spawner-kill shares) and shows it in spawner tooltips. Players who want it on screen install a mod (EvoGUI).

**Ours.** DECISIONS §5 and §6 put four global numbers in that slot: **Field** (W, in field-years since 1945, displayed as a year), **Junk share** (j), **Synthetic share** (σ) and **Leak** (03's accumulator L). They have no position (B L8), so they get no map layer. They get a strip, a window and a command. The strip is a BEND (information only, EvoGUI's placement precedent); the command is ISO. The symbols never appear on screen (08 §10.1).

### 4.1 World strip (HUD)

```
Field 2000 · Junk 40% · Synthetic 31%
Leak 0.37 ▕███▌      ▏
```

| Entry | Appears when | Format | Tooltip (08 §4.7) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field | Always | Year, integer: `1945 + ⌊W⌋` | "Field 2000.6. The field's own work: 17.0 years. The Site's papers: 38.6 years." The two terms are W_own and W_pub, stored separately (01 §12.1) |
| Junk share | Once *Crawling* is researched (01 §7.1) | Percent, integer | "Share of new human writing that is junk. Set by the field's era." |
| Synthetic share | Once σ > 0 | Percent, by §0's table (one decimal below 10%) | "Share of new writing that is generated. Endpoints add to it. It never falls." |
| Leak | Once a deploying building exists and Runaways ≠ Off | Two decimals and a bar 0 → 1: a counter toward its threshold in the evolution factor's slot, not a morality meter (DECISIONS §41) | "At 1.00 the deployment with the largest share is compromised. Largest share: Endpoint at (412, −96), tier 2, Standard. Rate 0.8 per hour from 40 deployments. Verification 0 of 4." The tier and quality are the named building's own; the quality reads "?" before *Interpretability* (R5-gate D8). The location is a link: click opens remote view there |
| Field run (race modes only) | Race or Race, hard | "2035 in 0:42:10" after the Field entry: the field's projected time to W = 90 at its current pace (03 §6) | "The field's run completes when it reaches 2035." |
| Runaway evolution (*Wild* only) | After the first nest | "Runaway evolution 12%" (03 §3.6) | Its three terms (time, clearing, escapes), uncommented |

- In the slice only Field shows (j is constant and nothing taps an exchange; σ = 0; no deployments).
- The strip sits in the right-hand column, which stays visible in remote view (I §4.11), so the numbers are also on screen in the map.
- Setting *Show world strip* (Interface, default on). Hidden, the numbers stay in the World window and `/world`.

## 5. Technology screen and research widget (I §4.9, §4.10)

### 5.1 Header readouts (DECISIONS §13)

A one-line strip above the research queue (left column of I §4.9):

```
Literature 1955 · Field 1951 · 4 years ahead
```

| Readout | Definition | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Literature** | The latest citation year among researched `regime: cited` technologies, trigger technologies included (01 §12.1, DECISIONS §35). A technology's year is the latest among its citations. `infrastructure` and `beyond` technologies never count | Year, or "—" before any cited technology |
| **Field** | `1945 + ⌊W⌋` | Year |
| **Gap** | The Site's year − Field. The Site's year = ⌊max(Literature, the `field_year` of researched `beyond` technologies)⌋, per 01 §12.1, so the gap is a whole number. While every researched technology is cited or infrastructure, this is Literature − Field (DECISIONS §13) | "N years ahead", "N years behind", or "Level" when 0. Hidden while Literature is "—". Tooltip: "The Site's newest technology, against the field." |

Once technologies beyond the literature are researched, Literature stops while the gap keeps growing. The arithmetic stops adding up on screen; that is the only sign that the tree has left the literature, and nothing explains it.

After the loop closes, a second line appears. **Closure** is the first System card written by a Research hall (DECISIONS §16, 04 §1); the first frontier run is not closure. Everything "after closure" in this file hangs on that card.

```
Doubling time 1:10 · 40:00 → 20:00 → 5:00 → 1:10
```

- **Doubling time** and its **history string** are computed simulation-side, deterministically, and saved; 04 §9 defines the sampling, the window and the rule for appending to the history. Since R5-gate D11 the doubling time is measured against the running maximum of the research rate, and the history and *Fastest doubling* take values only at new maxima, so a dip (an empty queue, a blackout) never shortens either. The UI only formats them.
- Only the last eight history entries show; the full string is in the tooltip and the run report.
- **Duration format:** `H:MM:SS` from one hour, `M:SS` from one second, `0.99 s` below one second.

**End state.** When no finite technology remains:

```
Research complete. Literature 2024. Technologies beyond the literature: 29.
```

The numbers are computed: Literature as above, and the count of researched `beyond` technology levels, each level of a multi-level technology counted once (H §5.5 #3). With the current data that is 19 *Model generation* levels (6–24), *Model generation 25* and nine others: 29. 2024 is today's value under the two-year citation rule, not a constant.

### 5.2 Technology cards and the detail panel

- **Tooltip** (I §4.8.4) reads, in order: the definition clause (`desc`); the unlocks and effect lines, generated from the data (`meta.effects.<type>.line`, 08 §5.4); the technology's `note`, if any ("Does not apply to machines."); and the short citation on its own line, grey: `(Shannon, 1948)` (08 §9.1). The `desc` never restates an unlock or an effect.
- **Detail panel** (selected technology): the same, and under it a **References** block with the long form of each citation (08 §9.2), small grey text, selectable for copying. `beyond` and `infrastructure` technologies show neither a citation nor References. The difference between the two is never labelled.
- **Search** (I §4.9) also matches citation surnames, so "Shannon" finds *Information theory* and *Switching circuits*.
- ***Model generation 6–25*** (DECISIONS §11): the data holds the multi-level *Model generation* (6–24) and the capstone *Model generation 25*. Each card's effect line reads "Research per paper {mult}" ({mult} prints its own ×), the multiplier taken from the Returns-to-research setting (×2 at the default; 04 §4). 25 also unlocks the Final report. They cite nothing; the detail panel shows their Handbook note, which carries the Fifth Generation reference (08 §9.3).
- **Trigger technologies** show progress "23 / 50" (I BEND-4).
- **The hidden *Loop closed*** (`loop-closed`; 04 §5, R5-gate D9) has no card, no queue entry, no search hit, no completion toast and no research log line; the milestone line `Loop closed.` marks the moment instead (08 §8.4). It counts in no readout: not Literature or the gap, not the end state's counts, not the run report's Peak research rate. The cards and detail panels of the technologies that require it (*Model generation 6*, *Wafer-scale integration*, *Fusion power*, *Research scheduling*, *Two-phase cooling*, *Fleet scheduling*, *Sparse inference*) show, where the missing prerequisite's icon would be, the grey line `Available once the loop closes: the first System card written by a Research hall.` (08 §4.6). Before closure they cannot be queued: *Start research* refuses them with the same line, rather than queueing a prerequisite no one can research.

### 5.3 Research queue bylines

Each queue entry's tooltip ends with a byline: `Queued by: you` for entries the player queued, `Queued by: Researcher` for entries appended after *Research scheduling* (H §5.5 #5; 08 §1.1 records the "you" as a statistics category). Nothing else changes. P0: the byline exists from the start, so the later change has something to change.

After *Research scheduling*, a checkbox above the queue, **Researchers fill the queue** (default on), turns 04 §5.1's policy on and off. The policy (impl L6; R5-gate D10): while fewer than 7 entries are queued and at least one Researcher is installed, each check appends the first entry of `meta.endgame.takeoff_order` that is neither researched nor queued, once its prerequisites are all researched, and nothing while it waits. The queue therefore shows the takeoff in its intended order and never runs ahead of the next *Model generation* level. An entry that waits on a technology outside the order (*Fusion power* without *Nuclear power*) is passed over. The message log marks its completions `(Researcher)` (08 §8.4).

### 10.2 Power switch as tripwire (ISO entity)

Factorio's power switch GUI, unchanged: a manual toggle plus a circuit condition. Because the signal persists without power (§10.1), the switch stays open until the machine is clean. The tooltip of an open switch that is cleaning shows `Cleaning: 0:18`: 30 consecutive seconds without power clean a machine, and any tick with power restarts the count (03 §3.4). Undo, copy-paste and blueprints carry the condition (ISO).

Turret-shaped GUI (Factorio's gun turret: status, preview, ammo slot):

### 10.4 Rollback (P2, impl L2)

A compromised machine has a **rollback input**: a slot that exists only while it is compromised and accepts exactly one item of its rollback type, `checkpoint` when the infection's tier is 1–3, `frontier-checkpoint` when it is 4 (03 §3.4). The item is consumed on arrival and the machine is clean at once. No other slot changes what it accepts; a checkpoint given to a compromised Endpoint goes to the rollback input first. Every ISO way of putting an item into a slot works, as for an ammo slot:
1. click the outlined slot in the machine's GUI with the copy in hand;
2. fast transfer (Ctrl+LMB) of the copy onto the machine;
3. an inserter, enabled by circuit or not;
4. from remote view, an item request that construction agents deliver (I §4.6).

Flying text "Rolled back". Consumption is final (no undo).

### 10.6 Leak

In the World strip and window (§4). Each deploying building's info box shows its own "Leak share 0.31" (03 §3.2), so the next compromise is predictable to the building. ## 13. Frontier cluster, first run, run report, race result, credits

### 13.1 Frontier cluster GUI (rocket silo, I §4.6 kind)

Factorio's silo GUI with one change: the rocket-part progress bar is drawn as a **falling loss curve** (H, D: "Progress is shown as loss"). x is run shards installed (0 → 100), y is the displayed loss L(p) = L∞ + B·(1 + 9p)^(−α), p = shards / 100. Every run uses L∞ = 1.6, B = 1.81 and α = 0.75 (3.41 → 1.92), except the Final report run, whose curve starts where the others end and falls with the same shape: L∞ = 1.6, B = 0.32, α = 0.75, so 1.92 → 1.66 (coherence C8; impl P24; R5-gate D12h). Its log line reads `Run complete. Loss 1.92 → 1.66.` Display only: the curve is a deterministic function of progress.
- **The Trainer** (R5-play #2; display only, as above). For the three recipes that produce weights, *Checkpoint*, *Post-training payload* and *Final report*, the Trainer GUI's progress bar is the same falling loss curve, L∞ = 1.6, B = 1.81, α = 0.75, with p = the current craft's progress (0 → 1), restarting with each craft. Every other Trainer recipe (the controllers, the agents, the *Researcher*, *Copy weights*, *Analysis* and *Meta-analysis*) keeps Factorio's bar: they copy or test weights and train nothing. No text accompanies the curve.

### 19.2 Saves and storage (I §4.20 BEND-3)

| Concern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Where | IndexedDB for metadata and small stores; OPFS for save blobs where available, IndexedDB otherwise |
| Persistence | Call `navigator.storage.persist()` at the first save; if denied, show once in Settings → Other: "Saves may be cleared by the browser when storage is low. Export a save to keep it." |
| Quota | Show used and available storage on the main menu and in Load game. Before a save that would exceed the quota: refuse with the reason and offer export or deletion |
| Format | One file per save: a compressed binary of the simulation state plus a JSON header (name, build, map settings, game time, thumbnail, save description). Export and import as files (`<name>.save`). Import refuses a newer format with a message; older formats migrate forward |
| Autosave | I's defaults (5 min, 3 rotating slots). Also on `visibilitychange` → hidden, if the last save is older than one minute |
| Serialisation | The simulation worker copies its state at a tick boundary and an IO worker compresses and writes it, so the game stalls ≤ 100 ms (10 §8.4, §11) |
| Save description | The Load list shows 08 §8.5's line when the player's last action is at least ten minutes older than the save; otherwise game time and current research |
| Run history | A small per-browser store, separate from saves: completed runs (for §13.3's dots), achievements, settings and keybindings. Exportable with the saves. Named **Run history** (R5-gate D4): "records store" is the in-world archive of 01 §6.2 |
| Two tabs | A `BroadcastChannel` lock: a second tab opening the game shows "The game is open in another tab." with [Take over] (the first tab saves and drops to a read-only notice) |
| Leaving | `beforeunload` asks for confirmation when unsaved progress is older than one minute |
| Offline | A service worker caches the build, so a loaded game keeps working offline; saves never leave the machine |

### 19.3 Background tabs

When the page is hidden, the game pauses (single player) and autosaves as above. Browsers throttle hidden tabs, so a background simulation would run irregularly; Factorio's own single-player pause on minimise is the precedent. Interface setting *Keep running in background tab* (default off) runs the simulation worker anyway, at whatever rate the browser allows.

## 23. Icons and sprites (D §12 in round 1, restated here; normative from M1)

Every item, fluid and technology icon, and every entity sprite, follows these rules. The data's `sprite` lines describe each one under them; 10 §9 builds and checks them.

**Legibility at 32 px on a belt.**
1. **Two palettes.** Matter keeps Factorio's metals and board colours. Information is paper, reels and drives: cream, manila, white sheets; grey drives with one coloured label. A belt's colour says whether it carries matter or information before any icon can be read.
2. **Families share one silhouette; type is colour, tier is one detail** (Factorio's practice):
   - Papers: one sheet silhouette in Factorio's seven science colours.
   - Modules: Factorio's card and colours (blue speed, orange productivity, green efficiency, and the quality module's colour for the Comparator); chevrons for tier.
   - Envelopes: Benchmark (a green check grid) and Adversarial set (a hazard stripe).
   - Reels and drives: **Dataset is a single labelled tape reel** (cyan label), a Corpus is three reels; a blank Drive is grey; filled drives carry a band in their fluid's colour.
   - **Weights are a bare hexagon chip**, not a drive: Checkpoint orange, Frontier checkpoint gold.
   - Chips: a green board with a tube → a black DIP chip on a red board → a gold-lid package ringed by memory stacks.
   - **Results:** Significant result is a sheet with a bright green band; Null result a plain sheet; Baseline the same sheet with the band greyed.
3. **A hexagon means weights.** It appears on the Checkpoint, the Frontier checkpoint, both controller families, the Researcher and the payload, and nowhere else (the Compromised warning icon's offset double hexagon, §10.1, is the one deliberate echo); Coprocessors (hardware only) carry none. The staffing map marks Researcher-staffed Seminars with it (§3.3).
4. **Fluids.** Data runs in flat black cables with a content stripe; physical fluids run in grey pipes. Data fluids copy oil's brightness order, so "heavier is darker" transfers: Crawl near-black with static, Junk muddy brown, Text amber, Prose pale ice-cyan.
5. **Ore extremes.** Quartz is the brightest raw item and Silicon the darkest plate (a violet sheen). Records are the only soft-edged raw item.
6. **Tested, not argued.** Every sprite is checked at 32 px against its belt neighbours before it is accepted: a belt line-up sheet of every item at 16 and 32 px, and a confusability report that flags any pair of items whose 8 × 8 Lab-colour thumbnails are closer than the threshold (10 §9.5). No tier may have more than eleven items sharing one silhouette.

**Projection and light** (10 §9.1 implements them): Factorio's oblique look (orthographic, 45° pitch, viewed from the south, 64 px per tile at High), a sun from the north-west so shadows fall east, desaturated colour with a warm key light and grime. Machines are drawn as what they are in their era: relays and tubes in the Memo tier, transistor racks in the Report and Article tiers, accelerator halls at takeoff; towns follow the field's era (01 §5.3).
